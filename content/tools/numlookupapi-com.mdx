You use Numlookupapi.com with a GET request and an API key from any language, or with the client SDKs the docs list. It suits developers who add phone checks to a sign-up form or clean a contact list.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan costs $0 a month and is meant for testing and personal projects. You get 100 requests per month, 1 API key, and a rate limit of 10 requests per minute. Batch validation is included, so you can send up to 100 numbers in one request.

Each lookup returns the carrier and line type, such as mobile, landline, or toll-free. It also flags VoIP and other special number types, which helps against fake sign-ups. The service covers more than 230 countries. The site says the free plan is enough to build and test your integration before you pay.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens after your 100 monthly requests are used. It does not mention overage charges on the free plan. The plan takes the form of a fixed allowance, so expect lookups to stop until the next month or until you upgrade.

The rate limit is separate. Paid plans have no rate limit, so more than 10 requests in a minute is a reason to upgrade. The paid plans start with Basic at $9.99 a month, and you can cancel from your dashboard.

## Gotchas

- 100 requests a month runs out fast in a live sign-up form. Treat the free plan as a test setup.
- The free plan has 1 API key. The Basic plan also has 1 key, and sandbox keys start at the next plan.
- Email support starts on paid plans. The free plan lists no support.

## Questions people ask

### Is Numlookupapi free?

Yes, up to a point. The Free plan costs $0 a month and includes 100 requests, 1 API key, and 10 requests per minute.

### What happens when I go over the Numlookupapi free limit?

The pricing page does not say. Paid plans start at $9.99 a month and add more requests, and they remove the rate limit.

### Does Numlookupapi need a credit card?

The pricing page does not mention a card for the free plan. You get a free API key from the sign-up button.
