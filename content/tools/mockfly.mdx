You build endpoints in the Mockfly dashboard, or import them, and your app calls the hosted HTTPS URL. A command line tool can also serve the same mocks offline. It suits front-end developers and QA engineers.

## What the free plan gives you

The Starter plan costs nothing and needs no credit card. It includes 500 requests per day, 1 project, 4 endpoints, and 2 responses per endpoint. Each project gets a namespace, and your mock API answers at `https://api.mockfly.dev/mocks/` followed by that namespace.

The paid plans remove these limits. Pro costs 15 EUR per month and has unlimited requests, projects, endpoints, and responses. It also adds callbacks, teammate invites, and AI generation of mock JSON. Team costs 49 EUR per month and includes 5 seats. The docs also say Pro and Team can serve a project on your own hostname.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens after the 500th request of the day. It only lists the limit and says Starter needs no credit card, so a bill is not possible on this plan. Free permits 1 project, 4 endpoints and 2 responses per endpoint. An import exceeding endpoint or response limits fails before creating the project. Upgrade to Pro for more. If you only need a short test, the offline command line tool can serve mocks you pulled earlier, but it needs a Mockfly account to pull them.

## Gotchas

- The free limit counts requests per day, so a busy test suite can use it up quickly.
- Two responses per endpoint limit how many conditional cases you can mock on the free plan.
- A project namespace starts as a random ID. Editing it to a readable name such as users is a paid Pro feature.

## Questions people ask

### Is Mockfly free?

Yes. Starter is free, with 500 requests per day, 1 project, 4 endpoints, and 2 responses per endpoint.

### Does Mockfly need a credit card?

No. The pricing page says Starter is free and needs no credit card.

### What happens when I go over the Mockfly free limit?

The pricing page does not say. Pro at 15 EUR per month removes the request, project, and response limits.
