You install Kiro as a desktop IDE, run it as a terminal CLI, or open it in a browser. It suits developers who plan features as specs and want a clear monthly credit cap.

## What the free plan gives you

The free plan costs $0 and gives you 50 credits per month. A credit is a unit of work for one prompt. A simple prompt can use less than 1 credit. A complex task, such as running a spec task, usually costs more than 1 credit. Credits are counted to two decimal places, so the smallest task costs 0.01 credits.

You get Claude Sonnet 4.5 and open-weight models, such as Qwen3 Coder Next, DeepSeek 3.2, and MiniMax M2.1. Access has rate limits. The pricing page says free access applies to people who sign in with a social login or an AWS Builder ID. You do not need an AWS account.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not describe the exact behavior when the 50 credits run out. It says paid plans can buy add-on credits to keep working. Those credits cost $0.04 each, and the page lists add-on credits only for paid plans. The free plan has no card on file, so no bill can follow.

The credit meter updates at least every 5 minutes in the subscription dashboard inside Kiro. Check it before long agent runs. Cheaper models use fewer credits for the same prompt.

## Gotchas

- Premium models, such as Claude Sonnet 5 and Claude Opus 5, need a paid plan.
- Kiro in GovCloud does not include the free tier.
- Upgrading to a paid plan needs a valid credit card. The first upgrade gives you a $20 credit toward the subscription.

## Questions people ask

### Is Kiro free?

Yes. Kiro Free costs $0 per month and includes 50 credits. It uses Claude Sonnet 4.5 and open-weight models, with rate limits.

### What happens when I run out of Kiro credits?

The pricing page does not say. It offers add-on credits at $0.04 each, but only for paid plans.

### Does Kiro need a credit card?

Not for the free plan. You sign in with a social login or an AWS Builder ID. A card is required only when you upgrade to a paid plan.
