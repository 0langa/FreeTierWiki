You sign in with your GitHub account and Hound reviews pull requests for you; there is nothing to install in your code. It suits open source maintainers who want style checks on every pull request.

## What the free plan gives you

The pricing section on the home page says Hound is free for public repos. Hound comments on code quality and style issues the moment a GitHub pull request is opened or updated.

The default style guides build on accepted industry standards, and the page says they come from years of use in software projects at thoughtbot. You can customize the guides to fit your team. The page lists no cap on the number of public repositories or reviews. It lists the supported languages as JavaScript, Ruby, Python, Swift, and more, and the help site has a page on supported linters.

## When you hit a limit

Public repositories have no stated limit, so nothing happens there. The limit is the type of repository. Private repos need a paid plan, and the plans count private reviews.

The page lists Chihuahua at $29 a month for up to 50 private reviews, Terrier at $49 for up to 300, Labrador at $99 for up to 1,000, Husky at $199 for up to 3,000, and Great Dane at $299 for more than 3,000. The page does not say what Hound does when a plan runs out of reviews.

## Gotchas

- Hound works on GitHub pull requests only, and you sign in with GitHub.
- Private repositories are paid, and the plans are priced by the number of private reviews.
- The help site says Hound does not support custom linters or rules.

## Questions people ask

### Is Hound CI free?

Yes for public repositories. The pricing section says Hound is free for public repos. Private repos need a paid plan.

### How much does Hound CI cost for private repositories?

Plans start at $29 a month for up to 50 private reviews. The top plan listed is $299 a month for more than 3,000 private reviews.

### Does Hound CI need a credit card?

The page shows no card step for public repositories. You sign in with GitHub.
