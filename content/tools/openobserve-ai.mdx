You can use OpenObserve through its web interface, over an HTTP API, or with the standard OpenTelemetry and log shippers. It suits developers who want one place for all three signal types.

## What the free plan gives you

There are three ways to run it. The open source edition is licensed AGPL-3.0, is free forever, and has no enforced usage limits on logs, metrics, or traces. The self-hosted Enterprise edition is free for up to 50 GB of ingestion per day, and it adds SSO, role-based access control, federated search, and an audit trail. The Cloud pricing FAQ advertises a no-card 14-day pay-as-you-go trial. Its official README instead mentions a Cloud free tier up to 50 GB/day. Those sources conflict; this entry relies on the explicit self-hosted offers for ongoing free use.

On self-hosted installs your own servers set the real limits. The cloud trial includes unlimited users, and all cloud plans have no limits on dashboards, alerts, or data sources.

## When you hit a limit

Cloud pricing markets a 14-day trial followed by metered ingestion and queries. Its displayed ingestion rate carries an annual-commitment discount footnote, so confirm billing interval and account terms. The README’s 50 GB/day Cloud allowance is not corroborated there. Neither source establishes automatic charging or data handling when a no-card trial expires.

Self-hosted Enterprise is licensed free up to 50 GB of ingestion per day; the enforcement mechanism at that threshold is not described. For more than that, you contact the sales team. The GitHub page also says you register at that volume. The open source edition has no stated ingestion cap, so your hardware and storage are the only limit.

## Gotchas

- Cloud pricing and README disagree about a permanent allowance. Self-hosted free licensing is supported by both pricing and downloads.
- All data is immutable once ingested. You cannot edit or delete single records, only drop whole retention periods.
- Cloud retention defaults to 30 days for logs and traces and 15 months for metrics. Extra non-metrics retention costs $0.02 per GB for each additional 30-day period.

## Questions people ask

### Is OpenObserve free?

Partly. The open source edition is free forever, and the self-hosted Enterprise edition is free up to 50 GB of ingestion per day. Cloud pricing advertises a 14-day trial, while its README describes a conflicting daily free allowance; confirm current account terms.

### Does OpenObserve Cloud need a credit card?

The pricing page says the 14-day free trial needs no credit card to start. Paid Cloud usage is metered, but the no-payment trial-expiration policy is not stated.

### Can I run OpenObserve on my own server?

Yes. The quickstart shows a single-node install with Docker, a downloaded binary, or Kubernetes. It listens on port 5080 and needs a root email and password on first start.
