You can use OpenObserve through its web interface, over an HTTP API, or with the standard OpenTelemetry and log shippers. It suits developers who want one place for all three signal types.

## What the free plan gives you

There are three ways to run it. The open source edition is licensed AGPL-3.0, is free forever, and has no enforced usage limits on logs, metrics, or traces. The self-hosted Enterprise edition is free for up to 50 GB of ingestion per day, and it adds SSO, role-based access control, federated search, and an audit trail. The hosted OpenObserve Cloud offers a 14-day free trial of the pay-as-you-go plan, and the pricing page says no credit card is needed to start.

On self-hosted installs your own servers set the real limits. The cloud trial includes unlimited users, and all cloud plans have no limits on dashboards, alerts, or data sources.

## When you hit a limit

On the cloud plan, the trial lasts 14 days. After that you pay as you go: $0.50 per GB ingested and $0.01 per GB queried. The pricing page does not say what happens to your data if the trial ends and you add no payment.

On self-hosted Enterprise, the free allowance stops at 50 GB of ingestion per day. For more than that, you contact the sales team. The GitHub page also says you register at that volume. The open source edition has no stated ingestion cap, so your hardware and storage are the only limit.

## Gotchas

- The cloud plan is a 14-day trial, not a permanent free tier. Only the self-hosted editions stay free.
- All data is immutable once ingested. You cannot edit or delete single records, only drop whole retention periods.
- Cloud retention defaults to 30 days for logs and traces and 15 months for metrics. Extra non-metrics retention costs $0.02 per GB for each additional 30-day period.

## Questions people ask

### Is OpenObserve free?

Partly. The open source edition is free forever, and the self-hosted Enterprise edition is free up to 50 GB of ingestion per day. The hosted cloud service gives a 14-day trial only.

### Does OpenObserve Cloud need a credit card?

The pricing page says the 14-day free trial needs no credit card to start. After the trial you pay for what you ingest and query.

### Can I run OpenObserve on my own server?

Yes. The quickstart shows a single-node install with Docker, a downloaded binary, or Kubernetes. It listens on port 5080 and needs a root email and password on first start.
