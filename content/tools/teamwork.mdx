Teamwork.com is a project management tool built around client work: projects are organized by client, and time is logged against tasks. A developer team would pick it when it builds for clients, such as a small agency or freelance group, and wants plans and hours in one place.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan is for small teams starting to bring more structure to their work, and it needs no credit card. It allows a maximum of 5 users and up to 5 projects. You can use task, list, board, Gantt, and table views on projects, log time, organize work by client, and connect chat tools.

The Free plan also lists 100 automations and US or EU data hosting. The Basics plan, at $9.99 per user per month billed yearly, adds unlimited templates and billable time tracking and reporting. Paid plans come with a 14-day free trial that also needs no credit card.

## When you hit a limit

The Free plan stops at 5 users and 5 projects. The pricing page does not say what happens when you reach either number, or when the 100 automations are used, beyond showing that paid plans raise them, with automation allowances in the tens of thousands on higher tiers.

The FAQ says you can upgrade your plan, add users, or buy add-ons at any time. If you later downgrade or remove users, the change takes effect at the start of your next billing cycle or renewal date.

## Gotchas

- Billable rates, invoicing from logged time, and profitability reports are paid features, though time logging itself is free.
- Five projects fill up fast if you create one per client.
- The page does not say whether the 100 automations reset each month.

## Questions people ask

### Is Teamwork.com free?

Yes. The Free plan needs no credit card and covers a maximum of 5 users and up to 5 projects.

### Can I track time on Teamwork.com Free?

Yes. The Free plan lets you log time; billable time reporting starts on the Basics plan.

### Is there a free trial of the paid plans?

Yes. Paid plans have a 14-day free trial, and Teamwork.com says no credit card is required.
