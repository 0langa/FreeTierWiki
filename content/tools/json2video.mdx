You use JSON2Video through a REST API, SDKs, or a visual editor in the dashboard. It suits developers and automation builders who produce many similar videos.

## What the free plan gives you

A free account comes with 600 credits and needs no credit card. Credits are consumed by the second. A standard video costs 1 credit per second of output, so a 30 second video uses 30 credits. At 1 credit per second, 600 credits is about 10 minutes of rendering. 4K video uses 4 times as many credits, so a 30 second 4K video uses 120 credits.

The credits are a one-time grant. Videos from the free plan carry a small JSON2Video watermark. The pricing page says external text-to-speech or transcription services may cost extra. Plans include video editing, templates, subtitles, voice-overs, and rendering, but no native AI image or video generation.

## When you hit a limit

When you run out of credits, the pricing page says you can buy a pre-paid plan to top up your account. A pre-paid plan is a one-time payment, and those credits do not expire. The first one costs $49.95 for 7,200 credits, which is up to 120 minutes of video. Subscriptions start at the Hobby plan with 3,000 credits per month, at $16.63 per month billed yearly. Subscription credits expire at the end of each month. On a free account, you top up by buying a plan.

## Gotchas

- The free credits are one-time. There is no monthly refill on the free plan.
- Free-plan videos have a watermark. Paid plans remove it.
- The pricing page says plans are sized in minutes of output video, and credits are used by the second. Long or 4K videos use credits fast.

## Questions people ask

### Is JSON2Video free?

Partly. A free account gives you 600 one-time credits and a free API key, with a watermark on the videos. No credit card is required.

### How many videos can I make with the JSON2Video free plan?

It depends on video length. A standard video uses 1 credit per second, so 600 credits cover about 10 minutes.

### Does JSON2Video have a watermark?

Yes, on the free plan. The pricing page says it was added because some users abused free accounts for commercial work.
