You sign in with GitHub, make a bucket, and point your requests at it. CurlHub then lists each request it receives. It suits one developer or a small team that debugs HTTP calls now and then.

## What the free plan gives you

The free plan includes 10,000 API requests per month. That is the only limit the pricing page shows for the free plan. The home page repeats the same number.

CurlHub lists the plans by monthly request volume. The next step up is 20,000 requests for $5 per month. After that come 100,000 requests for $10 per month and 1,000,000 requests for $20 per month. A yearly plan costs half as much per month on the smaller tiers. The pricing page also says the free tier will get more requests as more people sponsor the project on GitHub.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens after 10,000 requests. It does not mention a hard stop, a throttle, or a bill. The frontmatter keeps the free plan as a capped plan with no overage charge, and the pages do not contradict that.

The page shows no payment step for the free plan. You sign up with GitHub, and the sign-up page does not ask for a card. If you need more room, pick a paid plan. If you only debug now and then, the free volume is probably enough.

## Gotchas

- Sign-up needs a GitHub account. The sign-up page offers no other way in.
- The docs address at `docs.curlhub.io` returned a server error when we checked, so there is no working docs page to read.
- The pricing page lists volumes only. It says nothing about how long CurlHub keeps your logged requests.

## Questions people ask

### Is CurlHub free?

Yes. The free plan includes 10,000 API requests per month. Paid plans start at $5 per month for 20,000 requests.

### Does CurlHub need a credit card?

The free plan shows no card step. You sign up with a GitHub account.

### What happens when I go over the CurlHub free tier?

The pricing page does not say. It lists the 10,000 request limit and the paid plans, and it describes no overage charge.
