You work in the Flagsmith dashboard and read flags in your app through an SDK or the API. The code is open source, so you can also host it yourself. This entry suits solo developers and very small teams.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan costs $0. It includes up to 50,000 requests per month, 1 team member, 1 project, and API access. It also includes unlimited feature flags, unlimited environments, and unlimited identities and segments. A fair usage policy applies to the unlimited items.

The Free plan does not list A/B or multivariate testing, third-party integrations, scheduled flags, or two-factor authentication. The Start-Up plan adds these, plus 1,000,000 requests per month, 3 team members, unlimited projects, and email support.

The docs show SDKs for client-side and server-side apps, an API, and OpenFeature compatibility.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing FAQ says Flagsmith keeps serving flags to your users when you go over. It notifies you by email and in the app. On a free plan you then have 7 days to upgrade before any action is taken. The FAQ does not say what that action is.

Paid plans work differently. Overage charges apply, and additional API calls start at $50 per million per month. The Start-Up plan costs $45 per month, or $40 per month when paid yearly, and it has a 14-day free trial.

## Gotchas

- The Free plan allows only 1 team member and 1 project. You cannot invite a teammate without upgrading.
- Your app still gets flags after you pass 50,000 requests, but the 7-day clock to upgrade starts.
- The pricing page lists self-hosted and private cloud as enterprise options. The docs say you can also self-host with Docker.

## Questions people ask

### Is Flagsmith free?

Yes. The Free plan costs $0 and includes up to 50,000 requests per month, 1 team member, and unlimited feature flags.

### What happens if I go over the Flagsmith free limit?

Flagsmith keeps serving your flags. You get a notice and 7 days to upgrade before any action is taken.

### Can I self-host Flagsmith?

Yes. The quickstart says you can use the SaaS platform or a self-hosted instance, and it points to Docker instructions for self-hosting.
