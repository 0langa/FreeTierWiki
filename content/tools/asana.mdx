Asana is a task and project tracker with list, board, and calendar views. A developer would pick its free Personal plan to organize a side project or freelance work alone or with one partner, and connect it to tools such as Slack, Google Drive, and Zoom.

## What the free plan gives you

The Personal plan is $0 and listed as free forever, for one or two people managing personal projects. It allows 2 users and unlimited tasks and projects. You can flip between lists, boards, and calendars for the same work.

Storage is unlimited, with a 100MB maximum per file. Status updates are included, and Asana lists 100+ free integrations, naming Slack, Google Drive, and Zoom. The plan also includes a small allowance of Asana AI requests. The Starter plan, at $10.99 per user per month billed annually, removes the user seat limit and adds custom fields and unlimited free guests.

## When you hit a limit

The main limit is people. The Personal plan is built for 2 users; to add a third teammate you move to Starter or a higher plan, which the pricing page describes as having no user seat limits.

The pricing page does not say what happens if you try to invite a third member on Personal, and it does not describe any overage charges for the free plan. In practice the user count is the limit to plan around.

## Gotchas

- The 2-user cap makes Personal a solo or pair tool, not a team plan.
- Free guests, custom fields, and capacity planning are listed on paid plans only.
- AI request allowances are per user and per account, so heavy AI use runs out quickly.

## Questions people ask

### Is Asana free?

Yes. The Personal plan costs $0, is listed as free forever, and allows 2 users.

### How many people can use Asana for free?

Two. The Personal plan is for one or two people; larger teams need the Starter plan or above.

### Is there a file size limit on Asana Personal?

Yes. Storage is unlimited, but each file can be at most 100MB.
