Asana is a task and project tracker with list, board, and calendar views. A developer would pick its free Personal plan to organize a side project or freelance work alone or with one partner, and connect it to tools such as Slack, Google Drive, and Zoom.

## What the free plan gives you

The Personal plan is $0 and listed as free forever, for one or two people managing personal projects. It allows 2 users and unlimited tasks and projects. You can flip between lists, boards, and calendars for the same work.

Storage is unlimited, with a 100MB maximum per file. Status updates are included, and Asana lists 100+ free integrations, naming Slack, Google Drive, and Zoom. The Starter plan, at $10.99 per user per month billed annually, removes the user seat limit and adds custom fields and unlimited free guests.

## When you hit a limit

The Personal help page limits each project or team to two seats. If a project has more than two members after a downgrade, Asana says to remove members or upgrade to keep using it. Starter and higher plans list no user seat limit.

The pricing page does not say what happens if you try to invite a third member on Personal, and it does not describe any overage charges for the free plan. Asana's try-free account page says no card is required, with no separate Personal-specific wording.

## Gotchas

- The 2-user cap makes Personal a solo or pair tool, not a team plan.
- Free guests, custom fields, and capacity planning are listed on paid plans only.
- The pricing page lists Asana AI on paid plans; do not count paid AI allowances as part of Personal.

## Questions people ask

### Is Asana free?

Yes. The Personal plan costs $0, is listed as free forever, and allows 2 users.

### How many people can use Asana for free?

Two. The Personal plan is for one or two people; larger teams need the Starter plan or above.

### Is there a file size limit on Asana Personal?

Yes. Storage is unlimited, but each file can be at most 100MB.
