You connect repositories in the GitGuardian web dashboard and review incidents there. The open-source ggshield command-line tool runs locally, in CI, or as a pre-commit hook. It suits developers and small security teams.

## What the free plan gives you

The Starter plan costs $0 and is free always, with no credit card required. It includes internal secrets monitoring for up to 25 developers. You get unlimited real-time scanning and up to 500 historical scan detections. The pricing page also lists 10K API calls per month.

The comparison table lists a repository scanning capacity of 1 GB on Starter, against 12 GB on Growth and 60 GB on Enterprise. Starter has limited ggshield CLI and pre-commit hook support. Paid plans add endpoint protection as an add-on.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens when you pass 25 developers, 500 historical detections, or the API call limit. It lists no overage charge for Starter. Larger needs point to the Growth plan, which has up to 10 teams and extra features, and the Enterprise plan.

The page says you can start free and scan your first repo in 5 minutes, with no credit card.

## Gotchas

- Repository scanning capacity is 1 GB on Starter, so a very large repo may need a paid plan.
- ggshield sends files to the GitGuardian public API for scanning. The docs say only metadata is stored, and incidents from a ggshield scan do not show in your dashboard.
- Public secrets monitoring is limited on Growth and unlimited only on Enterprise, so the free plan does not watch public GitHub for your leaks.

## Questions people ask

### Is GitGuardian free?

Yes. The Starter plan is free always, with no credit card required, for up to 25 developers. It includes unlimited real-time scanning and up to 500 historical scan detections.

### Is ggshield free to use?

ggshield is open source. The docs say it works on macOS, Linux, and Windows, and it needs a GitGuardian API key to authenticate. The pricing page says Starter has limited CLI support.

### How big a repository can GitGuardian scan on the free plan?

The comparison table lists a repository scanning capacity of 1 GB on Starter. Growth lists 12 GB and Enterprise lists 60 GB.
