You use Cal.com in the browser: connect a calendar, create event types, and share the link. Developers can also use the API v2, an OAuth client, or embeds to put scheduling inside an app.

## What the free plan gives you

The Individuals plan is free forever and is meant for one person. The pricing page lists one user, unlimited event types and calendars, and email and SMS notifications, and it says you can start scheduling for free with no usage limits. The feature table also lists unlimited meetings, several duration options per event type, custom meeting locations, availability rules, time zone detection with conflict checking, and cancel and reschedule links for attendees.

Paid plans add team features such as shared availability, round-robin scheduling, and removing Cal.com branding. The Teams plan starts with a 14 day free trial.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not describe any usage cap on the free plan, so there is no stated throttle or bill. The practical limit is the number of people: the plan covers one user. If you need shared team availability, you move to the Teams plan, which has a trial first. A card is not required for the free plan, so you cannot be charged by accident.

## Gotchas

- Only templated workflows come with the free plan, so custom reminder flows may need an upgrade.
- The open source edition is now called Cal.diy. Its README says it is a self-hosted fork of Cal.com with enterprise features removed, has no hosted version, and is for personal, non-production use.
- Branding and custom sub-domain options sit in the paid feature list, so free booking pages may show Cal.com branding.

## Questions people ask

### Is Cal.com free?

Yes. The Individuals plan is free forever for one user, with unlimited event types and calendars. Paid plans are for teams.

### Does Cal.com need a credit card?

No. The site says no credit card is required, and you can sign up with Google or email.

### Is Cal.com open source?

Partly. The community edition, Cal.diy, is on GitHub under the MIT license for self-hosting. The hosted Cal.com has paid enterprise features that Cal.diy removed.
