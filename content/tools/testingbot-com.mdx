You run tests from your own machine or CI, pointed at the TestingBot grid. A web dashboard shows each test with video and logs.

## What the free plan gives you

Signup advertises a no-card 28-day trial: 100 testing minutes and 1 parallel test for live, automated, and visual testing. Pricing instead says 14 days; the homepage describes an ongoing Free plan plus a 7-day card-required full-access trial. Confirm your account terms because these offers conflict.

Open source projects can apply for a separate free plan. It offers unlimited automated tests with no monthly cap and 2 parallel sessions, on 6100+ browsers and devices, including real iOS and Android devices. To qualify, the project must be public, non-commercial, actively maintained, and under an OSI-approved license. Create a free account and submit the repository for approval.

Paid plans start with Live at €20 per month billed annually, or €30 month to month. Automated costs €50 per month billed annually, and Automated Pro costs €90.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens when the trial ends, beyond needing to upgrade. For paid plans, the FAQ is clear. Once you reach your monthly testing time you can no longer run tests until your next billing month, or you can upgrade to a larger plan.

You can cancel at any time, but if you cancel mid-month you are charged for that month. Pay-as-you-go packs offer 1,000 minutes for €60 without a subscription. The pricing page also offers auto-renew, which automatically buys the pack again when credits are low; you can turn it off.

## Gotchas

- Published durations disagree: signup says 28 days, pricing says 14, and the homepage FAQ says a 7-day card-required full-access trial. The signup offer explicitly says no card.
- The open source plan is not automatic. Your project is reviewed, and it must be actively maintained with recent commits.
- A parallel test is a test running at the same time. The open source plan lists 2 parallel sessions, and the Automated plan lists 1 parallel test.

## Questions people ask

### Is TestingBot free?

Signup advertises a no-card 28-day trial, but other official pages give different durations. Qualifying open source projects can apply for an ongoing free plan.

### Does TestingBot have a free plan for open source?

Yes. Open source projects can apply for unlimited automated testing with 2 parallel sessions. The project must be public, non-commercial, and under an OSI-approved license.

### What happens if I use all my monthly testing time?

You can no longer run tests until your next billing month, or you can upgrade to a larger plan.
