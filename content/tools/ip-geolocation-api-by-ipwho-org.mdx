You get a free API key from the dashboard, then call `api.ipwho.org` with a plain GET request or use an official SDK. It suits developers who want every data field, including VPN and Tor flags, on a free key for a personal or non-profit project.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan costs $0, needs no card, and gives you 2,000 lookups per day. It has no feature gating: you get location, time zone, currency, ASN, ISP, security flags, bulk lookup, and the client SDKs. The pricing page lists 16 global data centres and no support on the free plan.

The rate-limit docs describe the free plan as for personal, testing, and non-profit use, and the pricing page calls it perfect for testing. The Launch Special plan costs $2.50 a month billed yearly ($3 billed monthly) for 5,000 lookups per day. The Startup plan costs $10 a month billed yearly ($14 billed monthly) for 250,000 lookups per day.

## When you hit a limit

Two limits apply. The daily quota is a hard limit, and past it the API returns HTTP 429 with the code `daily_quota_exceeded`. There is also a burst cap of up to 100 requests per second on every plan, which returns 429 with `burst_limit_exceeded`.

The pricing page says repeated overages will require an upgrade. The docs say free users who hit the daily quota may be prompted to upgrade. Nothing in these pages bills a free key for extra requests.

## Gotchas

- The docs describe the free plan as for personal, testing, and non-profit use, while the FAQ calls it suited to smaller production apps. Ask IPWho before relying on it for a commercial project.
- The product is now called IPWho and runs on the Lavrox network, so you may see both names.
- The free plan has no support. The pricing page lists priority support on Launch Special and email support on Startup.

## Questions people ask

### Is IPWho free?

Yes. The Free plan costs $0, needs no credit card, and allows 2,000 lookups per day with all features.

### What happens when I go over the IPWho free limit?

The API returns HTTP 429 with `daily_quota_exceeded` until the next day. Hitting the limit may prompt you to upgrade.

### Can I use IPWho in a commercial project?

Not clearly. The rate-limit docs describe the free plan as for personal, testing, and non-profit use, and the vendor's llms.txt lists full commercial usage on Startup. The FAQ says the free tier suits smaller production apps, so confirm with IPWho first.
