You get a free API key from the dashboard, then call `api.ipwho.org` with a plain GET request or use an official SDK. It suits developers who want every data field, including VPN and Tor flags, on a free key for testing or a small project.

## What the free plan gives you

Free costs $0 without a card for 2,000 daily lookups, including location, time zone, currency, ASN, ISP, security flags and SDKs. Pricing/signup include bulk access, while its landing page says all paid plans; confirm eligibility. Pricing lists 16 global data centres without Free support.

Documentation targets testing/small projects, and FAQ mentions smaller production apps. Earlier noncommercial restrictions are absent, but current commercial licensing remains undefined.

Launch Special costs $2.50/month annually or $3 monthly for 5,000 daily lookups. Startup costs $10/month annually or $14 monthly for 250,000 daily lookups. Paid capacity does not resolve the ambiguous Free licensing statement.

## When you hit a limit

Two limits apply. The daily quota is a hard limit, and past it the API returns HTTP 429 with the code `daily_quota_exceeded`. There is also a burst cap of up to 100 requests per second on every plan, which returns 429 with `burst_limit_exceeded`.

The pricing page says repeated overages will require an upgrade. The docs say free users who hit the daily quota may be prompted to upgrade. Nothing in these pages bills a free key for extra requests.

## Gotchas

- Confirm commercial licensing for your use case. The current small-project wording does not explicitly grant it or repeat the old noncommercial restriction.
- The product is now called IPWho and runs on the Lavrox network, so you may see both names.
- The free plan has no support. The pricing page lists priority support on Launch Special and email support on Startup.

## Questions people ask

### Is IPWho free?

Yes. The Free plan costs $0, needs no credit card, and allows 2,000 lookups per day with all features.

### What happens when I go over the IPWho free limit?

The API returns HTTP 429 with `daily_quota_exceeded` until the next day. Hitting the limit may prompt you to upgrade.

### Can I use IPWho in a commercial project?

The current docs describe testing and small projects, and the FAQ mentions smaller production apps. They no longer repeat the former noncommercial restriction, but do not explicitly define commercial rights either. Confirm with IPWho for your intended use.
