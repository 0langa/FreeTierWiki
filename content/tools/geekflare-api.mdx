You send requests with one API key from any language, or try them first in the playground. Logs show every call. It suits developers and AI agent builders who need web pages as clean data.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan costs $0 per month and needs no card. It gives you 500 credits per month, 1 team member, 7 days of log retention, and 1 request per second. All 13 endpoints share one credit pool and one API key.

Credits are charged by endpoint. Web scraping, meta scraping, DNS lookup, and redirect checks cost 1 credit. Search costs 2. Screenshots and PDFs cost 5. Failed requests cost nothing.

The scraping endpoint renders JavaScript when a page needs it. CAPTCHA solving and anti-bot bypass are included in the base scraping price. The other endpoints cover load time, TLS scans, broken link checks, Lighthouse, port scans, and site status.

## When you hit a limit

The free tier renews every month at 500 credits. If they run out, you can wait for the renewal. You can also pay for a plan or buy a credit pack. Paid plans start at $19 per month. Credit packs start at $10 and need no subscription.

Speed has its own limit. Free allows 1 request per second, Starter 5, Growth 10, and Business 25. For more, the company asks you to contact them. The page does not say what happens when you go over the rate, so add a delay between calls.

## Gotchas

- Credit use depends on the endpoint. A batch of screenshots drains the 500 credits five times faster than scrapes.
- The Free plan keeps logs for 7 days. Starter and Growth keep them for 30, and Business for 90.
- The Free plan has one team member. Starter has 3 and Growth has 5.

## Questions people ask

### Is the Geekflare API free?

Yes. The Free plan gives 500 credits per month with no card. It renews every month.

### What happens when Geekflare free credits run out?

The free tier renews every month at 500 credits. Paid plans start at $19 per month, or you can buy a credit pack from $10 without a subscription.

### Do failed Geekflare API requests use credits?

No. The site says failed requests cost nothing.
