You call UniRateAPI with a GET request and an API key from any language. The docs also show an official Python client that you install with pip. It suits developers who add currency conversion to apps, shops, and dashboards.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free tier costs $0 a month, lasts forever, and needs no credit card. The site calls it good for testing and personal projects. You get 200 requests a day, which the pricing page puts at 6,000 a month, with a limit of 30 requests per minute. The pricing page lists 170+ fiat currencies, and the home page says the free tier also covers cryptocurrencies, with more than 870 currencies in total.

The home page says EU VAT rates are included on the free tier. The pricing page says the free tier has no historical data access, so treat history as a Pro feature.

## When you hit a limit

If you pass the daily limit, API requests return a 429 error. The error includes information about when your limit resets, which is midnight UTC. You can upgrade to Pro for higher limits.

Because the free tier needs no card, nothing is billed. Pro costs $9 a month and gives 25,000 requests a day, historical data from 1999, and time series data.

## Gotchas

- Historical rates, precious metals, energy and farm commodity prices, and VAT number validation need the paid Pro plan.
- The per-minute limit of 30 requests applies on top of the daily limit, so a burst can fail before the day ends.
- The home page and pricing page word the feature lists differently. Check the pricing page before you rely on a feature.

## Questions people ask

### Is UniRateAPI free?

Yes. The Free tier costs $0 forever and allows 200 requests a day. It needs no credit card.

### What happens when I go over the UniRateAPI limit?

Requests return a 429 error until the limit resets at midnight UTC. You can upgrade to Pro for higher limits.

### Does UniRateAPI give historical exchange rates for free?

No, according to the pricing page. Historical data back to 1999 is a Pro feature at $9 a month.
