StackBlitz is an online IDE that runs Node.js toolchains inside the browser tab, using its WebContainers technology, so a project starts without a remote server. Developers use it to try frameworks, share demos, and post runnable bug reproductions. The same company makes Bolt, an AI app builder, which has its own pricing.

## What the free plan gives you

The Personal plan costs $0 a month and you sign in with GitHub. It includes StackBlitz's fully browser-based IDE, unlimited public projects, the ability to open and edit public GitHub repositories, up to 1MB of file uploads per project, and community support. The plan also includes the features of Bolt Personal.

The pricing FAQ says you do not need a GitHub account to view and edit StackBlitz projects, but you do need one to save your work or collaborate. Free users in a team can access and edit all public projects and GitHub repositories.

## When you hit a limit

The free plan's limits are about what you can reach, not about usage. Private repositories, private collections, and repositories owned by a GitHub organization need a paid Teams seat, which is $55 per member per month billed annually or $60 billed monthly, for teams of up to 10 users.

Pro, at $18 a month billed annually or $25 billed monthly, removes the 1MB upload cap and lets projects connect to backends and APIs running on localhost and to CORS-protected APIs. The pricing page does not describe usage charges on the Personal plan.

## Gotchas

- Everything on the free plan is public, so do not paste secrets or private code into a Personal project.
- The 1MB upload cap per project rules out large assets such as videos or datasets.
- Calls from the browser IDE to an API on your localhost need Pro, which surprises people testing against a local backend.

## Questions people ask

### Is StackBlitz free?

Yes. The Personal plan includes unlimited public projects and public GitHub repositories with no monthly cost.

### Can I use private repositories on the free plan?

No. Private collections and organization repositories need a paid Teams seat, and Teams members must be paid users to reach private repositories.

### Do I need a GitHub account?

Only to save your work or collaborate. The FAQ says you can view and edit projects without one.
