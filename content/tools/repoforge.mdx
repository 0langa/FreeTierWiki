You publish with your usual tools, such as twine, and install with pip against a registry address RepoForge gives you. There is nothing to host yourself.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan is meant for open source projects and costs nothing. It includes 100 MB of storage and unlimited public packages. The plan is free forever and needs no credit card. The site lists Python, Docker, NPM, Debian, and Conda as supported formats.

The pricing page says plainly that the Free plan is not for commercial use. If your project is a business, plan to use a paid tier.

Paid plans are priced per user per month in pounds. Basic costs £12 per user and adds 1 GB of storage per user, unlimited private packages, and REST API access. Standard and Advanced raise storage further and add mirroring, SSO, and custom domains.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens when you use all 100 MB of storage. The site says there are no bandwidth caps and no surprise invoices, but it does not describe what a full registry does.

The site shows no card step for the Free plan, so a bill can only start when you choose a paid plan. Paid plans are priced per user, and storage on them is counted per user, so check the plan table before you add people.

## Gotchas

- Free is for open source projects only. The pricing page lists "Not for commercial use" under the Free plan.
- Private packages are not part of Free. The Basic plan is the first one with unlimited private packages.
- Storage on paid plans is counted per user, so the total grows with your team size.

## Questions people ask

### Is RepoForge free?

Yes, for open source projects. The Free plan gives you 100 MB of storage and unlimited public packages, and it is free forever.

### Does RepoForge need a credit card?

No. The site says no card is needed to start free. You only pay if you move to a paid plan.

### Can I use RepoForge for private packages for free?

No. The Free plan is for public packages. Unlimited private packages start on the Basic plan.
