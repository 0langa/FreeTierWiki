Budibase is an open source platform for building internal apps, automations, and AI agents on top of your data. You can self-host it for free or use Budibase Cloud. This entry covers Budibase Cloud, which offers a trial rather than a free plan.

## What the free plan gives you

Budibase Cloud has no always-free plan. It starts with a 14-day free trial and costs from $299 per month. Cloud includes unlimited apps, agents, automations, and requests, 2 creators, 20 end users, 250K monthly actions, and 50K monthly AI credits.

Usage is measured in actions: an agent tool call, an automation node run, or saving or deleting a row. Custom SQL queries and API requests are not counted as actions. Budibase also offers a self-managed Community plan at $0 that is fully open source and free to use indefinitely, with unlimited users, apps, agents, automations, and requests.

## When you hit a limit

Budibase says actions are hard monthly limits based on your selected Cloud allowance. When you reach the limit, you need to increase that allowance, through sales, to continue running agents and automations. Budibase chose hard limits over overage billing so costs stay predictable.

The pricing page does not say what happens when the 14-day trial ends, or whether the trial asks for a card. Paid Cloud customers can buy extra AI credits as one-off purchases, from 10,000 credits for $20.

## Gotchas

- There is no free hosted plan; after the trial, staying free means self-hosting.
- Cloud starts at $299 per month, so the step up from self-hosting is large.
- Extra end users cost $5 each per month on yearly billing, and extra creators cost $50 each.

## Questions people ask

### Is Budibase free?

The self-hosted open source edition is free to use indefinitely. Budibase Cloud is paid, from $299 per month, with a 14-day free trial.

### Are Budibase actions a hard limit?

Yes. Actions are hard monthly limits, and when you reach yours you need a higher allowance to keep running agents and automations.

### Do API requests count as Budibase actions?

No. Custom SQL queries and API requests are not counted as actions.
