You manage aliases in the web dashboard. There is also a browser extension, a command-line tool, a REST API, and an MCP server for AI tools. It suits people who want privacy without running their own mail setup.

## What the free plan gives you

The free plan costs $0. It includes 10 email aliases, 1 custom alias, and 1 email recipient. It also includes PGP encryption for forwarded copies, replies from an alias, and 500 API requests per month. The alias page says known tracking pixels and tracking parameters are removed from forwarded HTML email.

Custom domains are not part of the free plan. The pricing page lists them for Plus and Pro. Plus raises the limits to 100 aliases and 10 custom aliases, and Pro offers unlimited aliases and 100 custom aliases.

The same account also covers file drops and forms. This entry covers only the Alias part.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing FAQ says anon.li does not charge overage fees. When you reach a limit, you have to upgrade or delete existing items before you create new ones. Your existing aliases and drops keep working.

The pricing page does not say what happens when you use more than 500 API requests in a month. Plus and Pro raise that allowance, so heavy script users should look at those plans first.

## Gotchas

- Sign-up uses a magic link sent to your email, and you may be asked to set a vault password afterwards.
- A disabled alias rejects all future mail to it. You do not need to unsubscribe from anything.
- The pricing page says refunds are generally not given, so check a paid plan before you buy it.

## Questions people ask

### Is anon.li Alias free?

Yes. The free plan costs $0 and includes 10 email aliases, 1 custom alias, and 1 recipient. It needs no credit card.

### What happens when I reach the anon.li alias limit?

The site charges no overage fees. You must upgrade or delete existing aliases to create new ones. The aliases you already have keep working.

### Can I reply from an anon.li alias on the free plan?

Yes. The free plan lists replying to emails. Your reply goes out through the alias, so the recipient sees the alias and not your real address.
