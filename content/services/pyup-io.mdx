You use Safety from a terminal: install the CLI with pip, sign in through your browser, and run a scan in your project folder. A GitHub Action is available for CI/CD. It suits Python developers who want a free dependency check.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan costs $0 and the pricing page says it is free forever. It is designed for non-commercial use, individual developers, and education. You get a single codebase and 100 scans per month for a single user. The plan includes project-specific security policies, vulnerability management and prioritization, and email support with a 48-hour response.

Free scans use public vulnerability data. A scan reads your project folder for package files and virtual environments, checks the packages against known vulnerabilities and malicious package lists, and gives fix recommendations. Output can also be JSON.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens after your 100th scan in a month. The plan has a fixed scan cap, and the page mentions no card or pay-as-you-go charge for it. If you need more, the Team plan lists 5,000 scans per month, up to 25 codebases, and up to 20 contributing developers. It costs $25 per developer per month, or $240 per year.

## Gotchas

- The Free plan is for non-commercial use. A work project or a team needs a paid plan.
- Free scans use public vulnerability data. Paid plans use Safety's own data, which the pricing page calls 4x more comprehensive.
- Firewall protection, which blocks bad packages at install time, is a paid feature.

## Questions people ask

### Is Safety (PyUp) free?

Yes, for one developer. The Free plan covers a single codebase and 100 scans per month for non-commercial, individual, and education use.

### Can I use Safety free for commercial projects?

The pricing page says the Free plan is designed for non-commercial use. Commercial or team work belongs on the Team plan.

### How do I run my first Safety scan?

Install it with `pip install safety`, run `safety auth login`, then run `safety scan` in the root folder of your Python project.
