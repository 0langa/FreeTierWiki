QuickChart turns a Chart.js configuration into an image you can embed anywhere, including emails and PDFs where JavaScript does not run. It also generates QR codes, word clouds, and tables. The project is open source under AGPLv3, and QuickChart also sells a hosted paid service.

## What the free plan gives you

The Community plan costs $0 a month, and the pricing page marks it as no purchase required. It renders charts and QR codes with no watermarks, using any Chart.js config, with options such as multiple axes, gradient and patterned charts, and retina support.

The free service runs on shared servers with variable latency, best-effort email support, and no SLA. The FAQ says charts generated by the free version are public domain, so you are free to use them for any purpose, while paid users keep full copyright to their images. QuickChart says chart data is deleted as soon as it is rendered.

## When you hit a limit

Free requests are rate limited. When you go over, the API returns HTTP 429 Too Many Requests; the error guide says to wait before retrying, use the Retry-After header when present, and increase the delay after repeated failures. Nothing is billed on the free plan.

The pricing page does not publish the free rate limit as a number. For higher limits you authenticate with a paid API key: the Professional plan costs $40 a month, lists 10,000 images a month and 50,000 short URLs a month, and adds low-latency dedicated servers and priority support.

## Gotchas

- The rate limit covers chart rendering, validation, QR reading, word clouds, tables, and watermarking alike.
- Some Chart.js internals and custom plugins are restricted on the free tier.
- Long chart configs can hit URL length limits set by browsers and hosts, so consolidate the payload or reduce the data points.

## Questions people ask

### Is QuickChart free?

Yes. The Community plan costs $0 a month with no watermarks, but free requests are rate limited.

### Can I use free QuickChart images commercially?

The FAQ says charts generated by the free version are public domain, so you can use them for any purpose.

### What happens if I send too many requests?

You get HTTP 429 Too Many Requests. Wait before retrying and use the Retry-After header when present.
