You use a client library in the browser or app to connect, subscribe to rooms, and publish messages, and a server library or REST call to publish from your backend. It suits small apps that want realtime features with little setup.

## What the free plan gives you

The free plan allows 20 concurrent connections and 100,000 events per day. A concurrent connection is one WebSocket or fallback connection, usually one per browser tab or app. The pricing page says all features are available on every plan, including the free one.

An event is each message and each observable event sent and delivered. If one user sends a message to 10 other users, it counts as 11 events. The home page lists realtime messaging, message history, and user presence as built-in features.

## When you hit a limit

Scaledrone does not cap your traffic right away. The pricing page says it notifies you by email and gives you seven days to upgrade to a bigger plan before it caps you.

The next plan up, Hobby, costs $19 per month for 100 concurrent connections. The pricing page lets you change your subscription plan at any time and says no credit card is required.

## Gotchas

- Each browser tab usually opens its own connection, so 20 connections go fast with a few testers.
- Event counts grow with the audience. One message to 10 users uses 11 events, so a busy room uses its 100,000 daily events faster than the number of sends suggests.
- Calling `drone.close()` ends the connection, and Scaledrone does not reconnect automatically after the close event.

## Questions people ask

### Is Scaledrone free?

Yes. It is free for up to 20 concurrent connections and 100,000 events per day, with all features included.

### What counts as an event on Scaledrone?

Each message and observable event that is sent and delivered. A message from one user to 10 other users counts as 11 events.

### What happens when I exceed the Scaledrone free limit?

Your traffic is not capped right away. Scaledrone emails you and gives you seven days to upgrade before it caps you.
