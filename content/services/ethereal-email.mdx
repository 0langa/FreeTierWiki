You create a throwaway account from the Nodemailer API or with one click on the website, then send mail over SMTP. You read the caught messages through a preview link, the website, or an IMAP or POP3 client. It suits developers who test email code.

## What the free plan gives you

Ethereal is completely free. The FAQ says it is run by the Nodemailer team and funded by EmailEngine, a commercial email API.

You can create as many test accounts as you want, even from code, with no signup or email verification. Ethereal accounts act like standard SMTP, IMAP, and POP3 accounts, so any mail library that speaks SMTP works. Every message is caught and stored for review. Nothing is delivered.

Public accounts can send up to 200 messages per hour. Each address has 100MB of message storage.

## When you hit a limit

At the send limit, the server temporarily rejects further messages until the start of the next hour. Accounts made by EmailEngine subscribers are not rate limited.

At the storage limit, the account cannot receive more messages. To free space, delete older messages with an IMAP client (POP3 does not work for this), or wait for them to expire. Messages are stored for only a few hours, after which they are deleted.

## Gotchas

- Message preview URLs are public and need no authentication. Anyone with the link can read the message.
- Inbound email is disabled for public accounts, and mail sent to those addresses bounces. Inbound email works only for accounts made by EmailEngine subscribers.
- Ethereal never delivers mail. Do not use it to send to real users.

## Questions people ask

### Is Ethereal Email free?

Yes. Ethereal is completely free to use. You can create as many test accounts as you want.

### Does Ethereal Email deliver emails?

No. It accepts mail over SMTP but never delivers it. You read the caught message by a preview URL or an IMAP client.

### How long does Ethereal keep my emails?

For a few hours, then it deletes them. Each address also has a storage quota of 100MB.
