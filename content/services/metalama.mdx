You add Metalama as a NuGet package, write aspects in C#, and the compiler applies them during the build. Visual Studio Tools for Metalama are optional. It suits .NET developers who repeat the same patterns in many places.

## What the free plan gives you

Metalama has two free editions. Metalama Open Source is free under the MIT license and includes all the open-source packages. Metalama Community is also free, and it adds closed-source productivity tooling. The pricing page says Community is for individuals, for companies of up to 3 users, or for non-commercial use.

Metalama follows an open core model. Most features are free and open source, while convenience features and extension packages need a commercial license. Metalama Professional costs $119 per user. Build servers are free, and the license never expires, though updates and support last 1 year.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not describe enforcement. It states who the Community edition is for, so the limit is about who you are rather than a quota. A company with more than 3 users needs a paid license. The docs also say architecture validation requires a Metalama Professional license. The pages ask for no card for the free editions, and the open source packages stay under the MIT license.

## Gotchas

- The Community terms depend on your situation: individual, non-commercial, or a company with up to 3 users. Check them before you ship commercial work.
- Architecture validation uses a separate package and needs a Professional license.
- Aspects run at compile time, so your source files stay unchanged and the effect shows in the built code.
