You work in a web-based Jupyter notebook. You create it from a template, pick a machine, and click Start notebook. Paperspace, now part of DigitalOcean, also sells paid machines you can use from the console, an API, and a CLI. The free plan suits learners who want to try a GPU notebook.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan costs $0. It has public projects, auto-shutdown with a 12 hour limit, and 5 GB of storage. It lets you use the free machine types, which are a Free CPU (C4) and a Free GPU (M4000). The docs say free machines give limited resources for development and testing, and only work for Notebooks in private workspaces.

The team free plan lists 10 GB of persistent storage, 10 notebooks in total, and 1 running notebook at a time, plus low instance types and public notebooks. Storage above the free amount costs $0.29 per GB per month. Paid plans start at $8 per month for individuals on the plan page.

## When you hit a limit

Notebooks shut down automatically at the 12 hour limit on the Free plan. Storage above the included amount is billed at $0.29 per GB per month.

If no free machine is available, your notebook stays pending in a queue until one frees up. The docs suggest a paid machine type if you need access right away. Machines outside the free tier are billed per hour. The FAQ says you can set billing alerts and limits on the billing tab.

## Gotchas

- Free machines depend on availability. The create-notebook page says a pending notebook waits for the next free machine.
- Free projects and notebooks are public. Private projects need a paid plan.
- Picking a paid machine, such as an A100 at $3.09 per hour, bills you per hour of use, separately from the plan fee.

## Questions people ask

### Is Paperspace free?

Partly. The Free plan includes a free CPU and a free M4000 GPU for notebooks, 5 GB of storage, and a 12 hour auto-shutdown.

### How long can a free Paperspace notebook run?

Free notebooks auto-shutdown after a 12 hour limit.

### Does Paperspace need a credit card?

The pricing page does not say a card is needed for the Free plan. Paid instances are billed per hour, and the FAQ says Paperspace takes payment by major credit and debit cards.
