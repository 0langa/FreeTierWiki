Fivenines is a hosted monitoring service that puts server metrics, network devices, uptime checks, and cron job monitoring in one dashboard. You install an open source agent on each server and add checks for websites and scheduled jobs. It suits developers and small MSPs who would rather not run Prometheus, an uptime checker, and a cron monitor separately.

## What the free plan gives you

There is no permanent free plan. The pricing page says to start with a 14-day Pro trial and pick a plan when you are ready, and that all paid plans include a 14-day free trial with no credit card required. The FAQ says every signup gets this trial.

Pro, the plan you try, includes 50 monitors, 10 team members, Slack and Teams alerts, 24 months of data retention, white-label status pages, and security vulnerability scanning. After the trial, monthly billing costs €19 for Starter (15 monitors, 3 team members), €49 for Pro, and €99 for Business. Annual billing shows monthly equivalents of €15, €40, and €79. Prices assume 60-second check intervals.

## When you hit a limit

The FAQ says there is no surprise charge at the end of the 14-day trial, because no credit card is required. You pick a paid plan during or after the trial to keep monitoring active, so without a plan your checks stop rather than bill. During the trial the Pro limits apply, including 50 monitors and 10 team members. You can switch plans or cancel at any time, according to the pricing page.

## Gotchas

- There is no free plan to fall back to after 14 days.
- The initially selected annual prices are monthly equivalents; paying monthly costs more.
- Prices exclude VAT where applicable.

## Questions people ask

### Is Fivenines free?

Not permanently. Every signup gets a 14-day trial with no credit card, then Starter costs €19 monthly or €15 per month billed annually.

### What happens when the trial ends?

The FAQ says there is no surprise charge; you pick a paid plan to keep monitoring active.

### Is the agent open source?

Yes. The pricing page describes it as an open-source agent.
