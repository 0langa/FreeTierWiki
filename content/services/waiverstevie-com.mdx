You turn a PDF into a waiver with an annotation tool, then collect signatures. You can also connect WaiverCat to your own app through its API. The free account is for testing, not for real use.

## What the free plan gives you

The free account needs no credit card. The pricing page describes it as a way to set up your environment and run tests. It includes API access. All PDFs from the free account are watermarked. That makes it a sandbox, not a free production plan.

Paid plans are simple. The Basic plan costs $15 per month, adds text and email notifications, and comes out to $0.06 per signature. The next plan costs $25 per month, adds 24-hour support, and comes out to $0.05 per signature. The site says an electronic waiver costs just $0.06, and that you can upgrade or cancel at any time. WaiverCat says more than 30,000 people have used it to sign PDF documents.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page lists no monthly cap or quota for the free account. It does not say what happens if you test heavily. The only stated limit is the watermark: free PDFs keep it until you upgrade. No card is on file, so the free account cannot create a bill. If you need clean PDFs, you have to move to a paid plan. The paid plans are priced per signature, so cost grows with use.

## Gotchas

- Every PDF from the free account is watermarked, so do not use it for real waivers.
- Text and email notifications come with the paid Basic plan, not the free one.
- The documentation host did not respond when we checked, so confirm API details inside your account.

### Is WaiverCat free?

There is a free account, but it is for setup and testing. It has API access, and all PDFs are watermarked. It needs no credit card.

### How much does WaiverCat cost?

Basic costs $15 per month, which works out to $0.06 per signature. The next plan costs $25 per month, or $0.05 per signature.

### Is WaiverStevie the same as WaiverCat?

Yes. WaiverCat is the new name of WaiverStevie. The pricing page is on waivercat.com.
