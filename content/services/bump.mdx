You use it from a web form and a Dashboard in the browser, and there is also a browser extension. It suits anyone who wants to give out a different address on every signup.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan costs $0 and is free forever. It includes 10 email aliases and 1 custom domain, and the pricing section says no credit card is required. Both plans list anonymous email reply and sending anonymous email. The site says Alias Email has no access to your inbox or email account. You can make an alias with the homepage form or from the Dashboard after you sign up.

The paid Premium plan removes the alias cap and allows 2 custom domains. It costs $3.33 per month when billed annually, and you can cancel for free within 14 days. Email support takes 2 to 3 days on both plans.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens when you reach 10 aliases or 1 custom domain. The only upgrade path it shows is Premium. The Free plan needs no card, so there is no bill to run up.

One related rule from the FAQ: the service says it will never store your emails for longer than three days. It holds mail briefly only if the service is down.

## Gotchas

- You can create a first alias from the homepage form, but sending from an alias uses the compose form in the Dashboard.
- The site does not say what the Free plan does when you pass 10 aliases, so delete aliases you no longer need.
- Custom domains are managed on their own page in the Dashboard, and the Free plan allows only one.

## Questions people ask

### Is Alias Email free?

Yes. The Free plan includes 10 email aliases and 1 custom domain, free forever, with no credit card required.

### What is the difference between Free and Premium on Alias Email?

Premium gives unlimited aliases and 2 custom domains. It costs $3.33 per month billed annually.

### Does Alias Email store my emails?

The FAQ says it will never store your emails for longer than three days, and only holds them briefly if the service is down.
