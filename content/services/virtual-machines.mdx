You create a VM in the Azure portal, pick Linux or Windows, and connect with SSH or remote desktop. It suits learners and small projects that need a full server for a year without paying.

## What the free plan gives you

New Azure customers get 750 hours each month of three burstable VM sizes: B1s, B2pts v2 (Arm-based), and B2ats v2 (AMD-based). The offer runs for 12 months after you create your account. It covers Linux or Windows VMs.

Azure shows this under the free services list of the Azure free account and of pay-as-you-go. The Azure free account also adds a $200 credit to use within 30 days, for services outside the free list. After that you must move to pay-as-you-go pricing within 30 days or after you use the credit, whichever comes first, to keep receiving free services.

## When you hit a limit

If you use more than the monthly free hours, or pick a VM size that is not on the list, Azure bills you at pay-as-you-go rates. The same happens after the 12 months end.

The free services FAQ says VMs you still run after 12 months keep running and are billed. So the offer does not stop your VM for you. Shut the VM down before the year ends if you do not want the charge. During the free account credit period, Azure says spending protection means your credit card will not be charged.

## Gotchas

- The 750 free hours apply to each listed size separately, and only those three sizes are eligible.
- Azure may place a temporary one-dollar authorization on your card during sign-up and remove it after verification.
- The $200 credit lasts up to 30 days. After that, move to pay-as-you-go to keep the free services.

## Questions people ask

### Is Azure Virtual Machines free?

For new Azure customers, yes, for 12 months. You get 750 hours each month of B1s, B2pts v2, and B2ats v2 VMs.

### What happens to my Azure free VM after 12 months?

It keeps running, and Azure bills it at pay-as-you-go rates. Delete or stop the VM before the year ends if you want to avoid charges.

### Does the Azure free VM need a credit card?

Yes. Sign-up verifies a card, and Azure may place a temporary one-dollar authorization that it removes after verification.
