You sign in with Twitter, add your URLs, and Pingbreak sends alerts as Twitter direct messages. Slack, Discord, Mattermost, mail, and webhooks are optional extras. It suits people who want simple, free up or down checks.

## What the free plan gives you

Pingbreak is a free open beta. The home page says beta testers can monitor an unlimited number of URLs, checked every minute. Alerts go out by Twitter direct message, and you can add Slack, Discord, Mattermost, mail, or webhook notifications as well.

The home page tells visitors not to look for prices, because the service is totally free. The team says current features will never require you to pay, and that all future updates will stay free for beta testers. Pingbreak says it does not sell your personal data.

## When you hit a limit

The home page does not state any limits, because the service lists no paid plan and no prices. There is no stated cap on monitors, and no billing, so there is no bill to fear.

The risk is different. The team writes that it does not know what the future holds, and the service is still in beta. It is built by volunteers and paid for by sponsors, so plan a backup alert if the site matters.

## Gotchas

- A Twitter account is required. The sign-in page is an X authorization screen that warns the app asks for sensitive permissions, so read the list before you accept.
- The service is beta, and the team says it is run by volunteers and sponsors.
- Alerts depend on Twitter direct messages first. Other channels are extras you add, and the home page lists no SMS or phone alerts.

## Questions people ask

### Is Pingbreak free?

Yes. The home page says Pingbreak is totally free, and beta testers can monitor an unlimited number of URLs with one-minute checks.

### Does Pingbreak need a Twitter account?

Yes. The home page says a Twitter account is required, and alerts arrive as Twitter direct messages. You can add Slack, Discord, Mattermost, mail, or a webhook on top.

### How often does Pingbreak check my site?

Every minute, according to the home page. It checks all of your websites on that interval and alerts you when one is down.
