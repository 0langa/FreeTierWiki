UserCheck, which used to be called MailCheck.ai, is an API that tells you whether an email address is disposable, a forwarding relay, or a likely typo. The old mailcheck.ai site now says MailCheck.ai is UserCheck.com.

## What the free plan gives you

The free plan costs $0 a month and is described as for small projects or trying out the service. It includes 1,000 credits per month, 1 request per second, a limited request log, unlimited user seats, multiple API keys, and UserCheck Gates. The page says you can sign up without a credit card.

One API call returns fields such as disposable, relay_domain, public_domain, free_subdomain, alias, and mx, plus the domain's MX records. That covers disposable email detection, forwarding detection, MX record validation, and typo suggestions. Integrations listed include the HTTP API, WordPress, and Laravel.

## When you hit a limit

The page counts credits per month but does not say what the API returns when the 1,000 free credits are used up. The free plan also caps you at 1 request per second, so a burst of sign-ups may need to queue checks.

The Pro plan costs $50 a month and raises the allowance to 200,000 credits per month and 5 requests per second. It adds a 30-day request log, bulk email and domain validation, a custom blocklist, and provider information. Larger plans are custom through sales.

## Gotchas

- The free request log is limited, while Pro keeps 30 days, so debugging a blocked sign-up is harder on the free plan.
- At 1 request per second you cannot validate a list in bulk on the free plan; bulk validation is a Pro feature.
- Old links, docs, and packages may still use the MailCheck.ai name for the same service.

## Questions people ask

### Is UserCheck the same as MailCheck.ai?

Yes. The mailcheck.ai site says MailCheck.ai is now UserCheck.com.

### Is UserCheck free?

Yes, within limits. The free plan includes 1,000 credits per month at 1 request per second, with no credit card required.

### What does UserCheck detect?

It flags disposable addresses, forwarding and relay domains, missing MX records, and common typos.
