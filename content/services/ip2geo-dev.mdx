You sign in to a cloud dashboard, create a project and a secret key, then call one REST endpoint or use an official SDK or CLI. It suits developers who want a typed SDK for IP lookups and a monthly allowance with no card.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan gives you 1,000 conversions every month, and you do not add a credit card. A conversion turns one IP address into location data. Each account starts with a default project, and the site says all plans, including the free one, get support by email.

The response includes the country, city, coordinates, timezone, currency, and EU flag, among other fields. Paid plans give more conversions: Starter has 10,000, Growth has 100,000, and Scale is unlimited. The site shows the Starter price as $10 a month.

## When you hit a limit

On a metered plan, once you reach the quota, further conversions return HTTP 429 with the message "Plan conversion limit exceeded". They stop until your limit resets or you upgrade. The CLI stops there, keeps what it already converted, and exits with code 5.

The docs say there are no strict rate limits per second, but the monthly quota is real. The site also says it may contact you if it sees unusual usage. The pages describe a 429 stop at the quota, not a bill, for the free plan.

## Gotchas

- You need an account to get a key. The service is not anonymous.
- A secret key (`i2g_sk_...`) is for server code. A public key (`i2g_pk_...`) is for client code, and websites must be registered for client-side use.
- Bulk calls use conversions too, so a long list can use the 1,000 quickly.

## Questions people ask

### Is ip2geo.dev free?

Yes, within limits. The Free plan gives you 1,000 conversions a month with no credit card. You do need an account.

### What happens when I go over the ip2geo.dev free limit?

Further conversions return HTTP 429 with the message "Plan conversion limit exceeded" until your limit resets or you upgrade.

### Does ip2geo.dev have an SDK?

Yes. The docs list official SDKs for TypeScript or JavaScript, Ruby, Python, and PHP, plus a CLI.
