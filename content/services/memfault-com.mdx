You add the Memfault SDK to your firmware, then follow devices and crashes in the Memfault web app. It suits embedded teams, especially those on Nordic chips.

## What the free plan gives you

The Developer plan is free per month. The pricing page describes it as exclusive pricing for teams building on Nordic low-power wireless solutions. It includes no charge for up to 10 development devices. The Get started button leads to nRF Cloud, so sign-up runs through Nordic's cloud pricing page. Memfault also offers an interactive sandbox on its docs site if you want to look around first.

Nordic restricts Developer to non-production use and shows resource limits by chipset. Its nRF54H configuration lists 14 days of data retention. Memfault's separate Growth offer starts at $3,495 per month and lists 30 days of retention.

## When you hit a limit

Memfault points teams beyond ten devices toward pay-as-you-go pricing. Nordic lists Pro from $0.10 per device per month, with separate charges for some services. The pages do not say whether Developer blocks an eleventh device or how you move to paid billing.

The page does not say whether a credit card is required. Nordic lists Developer only for non-production use.

## Gotchas

- The free plan is aimed at Nordic-based teams, so other hardware may not qualify.
- Production fleets need a paid plan, even when they have fewer than ten devices.
- Follow the current Nordic quickstart for account creation and your chip variant; older integration URLs may no longer resolve.

## Questions people ask

### Is Memfault free?

Partly. The Developer plan has no charge for up to 10 development devices, but the pricing is exclusive to teams building on Nordic low-power wireless solutions.

### What happens when I go over 10 devices on Memfault?

Nordic lists pay-as-you-go Pro for larger or production fleets, starting at $0.10 per device per month plus metered services. Developer's exact over-limit behavior is unpublished.

### How do I send data from my device to Memfault?

The nRF Connect SDK guide says devices push data chunks to the Memfault cloud. nRF91 and nRF70 devices can upload over HTTPS, and Bluetooth LE devices use a phone app.
