ZoneEdit is a DNS host that sells its paid service by credits. It also offers one free zone for a personal domain, which it describes as a way to try the service or host a personal project.

## What the free plan gives you

You can run a single personal domain on ZoneEdit at no cost. The free zone is for a non-commercial domain used for personal or academic purposes. It includes 100,000 DNS queries per month, which ZoneEdit calls adequate for most non-commercial use cases, 5 email forwarders, dynamic DNS support, and DNS-01 ACME and LEGO support for certificates.

Support on the free zone is community support, and there is no service-level agreement. To set it up, you verify your email, accept the acceptable-use terms, and point your domain at ZoneEdit.

## When you hit a limit

The Free DNS page lists 100,000 monthly queries, but its linked terms, dated June 2021, say free domains receive 250,000 and incur 2 credits per million beyond that. These conflicting policies leave the current free-zone threshold and credit charges unclear; ask support before relying on a no-charge ceiling.

The terms also allow suspension for unpaid DNS fees after reminders. Business use needs paid service. Paid credits cover zones and optional failover, backup MX, or a third nameserver.

## Gotchas

- Only 1 personal zone is free; a second domain needs paid credits.
- Commercial sites are outside the free terms, even if they are small.
- Free users must periodically confirm verification emails; failure can suspend the account. The signup card requirement is not separately published.

## Questions people ask

### Is ZoneEdit free?

Yes, for one domain. The free zone covers a single personal, non-commercial domain with 100,000 queries a month.

### Can I use the ZoneEdit free zone for a business?

No. The free zone is for personal or academic, non-commercial use, and ZoneEdit says business use should choose a paid plan.

### Does ZoneEdit's free plan support Let's Encrypt?

It lists DNS-01 ACME and LEGO support, which is how Let's Encrypt validates through DNS.
