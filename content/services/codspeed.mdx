You install the CodSpeed GitHub App, add benchmarks to your tests, and run them in CI. CodSpeed then comments on each pull request with a performance report. It suits library authors who care about speed.

## What the free plan gives you

CodSpeed's public sources disagree as of November 23, 2026. The rendered pricing page lists Free with unlimited runs and repositories, five users, three months of history, CI checks and reports, CPU and memory instruments, and 600 macro runner minutes per month. It lists Pro from $15 per user per month with unlimited history and a 14-day trial.

The linked billing documentation instead describes Hobby: five active contributors, three months of history, one concurrent Macro Runner job at low queue priority, and $50 of monthly usage credits shared by Macro Runners and the Optimization Agent. It describes Team at $500 per month, comprising a $250 platform fee and $250 in credits, with 20 contributors. Annual Team billing is $5,400 per year. These are conflicting published offers, not two allowances to combine.

## When you hit a limit

Under the Hobby policy in billing docs, work that needs credits pauses when credits run out until the next monthly grant or an upgrade. Unused monthly credits do not roll over. Gen 1 runners use $0.032 per minute, Gen 2 runners $0.064, and the Optimization Agent $2.50 per agent compute unit from that shared balance. Team continues beyond its credits and bills extra usage.

The pricing page instead lists 600 free runner minutes and Pro runner overage at $0.032 per minute. It does not explain Free enforcement past 600 minutes. Confirm which plan and meter applies before relying on either allowance; signup card requirements are also unpublished.

## Gotchas

- The pricing FAQ counts people who author private-repository pull requests or view detailed performance reports. Billing docs instead count commit authors/co-authors, pull-request authors, and reviewers/approvers over a rolling 30 days, once per GitHub account across the organization; bots are excluded.
- Pricing describes public OSI-licensed repositories as open source with unlimited users. Billing docs require an approved application for a non-commercial OSI-licensed project, then grant monthly credits, unlimited contributors, and unlimited history. Do not assume merely making a repository public qualifies.
- The published pages do not establish an effective date for the conflicting plan models. Check your account's plan and usage screen before budgeting a team rollout.

## Questions people ask

### Is CodSpeed free?

Both official sources offer a free plan with a five-person limit and three months of ordinary history. They disagree on its name, usage allowance, and definition of a counted person.

### How many free runner minutes do I get?

Pricing lists 600 per month. Billing docs instead give Hobby $50 in credits shared across runners and the Optimization Agent, so the number of minutes depends on runner generation and other usage. Confirm the applicable offer in your account.

### Does free usage stop at the limit?

Hobby billing docs say credit-consuming work pauses. The separate Free minute offer on the pricing page does not publish its excess-use behavior.
