You create an account, copy an API key from the dashboard, and call one HTTPS endpoint, `/v3/ipgeo`, from any language. It suits developers who want one call that returns location, currency, ASN, and time zone for testing or a small side project.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan costs $0 and gives you 1000 API credits per day, for life. The pricing page says no credit card is required. A successful lookup charges 1 credit, so the plan covers up to 1000 basic lookups a day.

The free plan returns location, country metadata, currency, ASN, and time zone. Lookup by domain, hostname, company data, security flags, bulk lookup, and translated responses need a paid plan. The first paid plan, Starter, costs $19 a month for 150000 credits. The page says to use the free plan only for testing and small projects, and the site says commercial use needs a paid plan.

## When you hit a limit

The free plan, which the docs also call the Developer plan, has no surcharge and no overage. It stays capped at its daily credits. The pricing page says there are no overages on Free. The docs name HTTP 429 for paid plans that have no overage; they do not spell out the free-plan response past the cap.

Paid plans can keep serving requests after their credits run out if overage is turned on, and they are billed in blocks. The free plan never bills you.

## Gotchas

- Credits are not requests. Adding security data to a lookup costs 2 more credits, so one call can cost 3 credits. Adding abuse contact data costs 1 more.
- Asking for a language other than English with a free key returns a 401 response.
- The free plan is for non-commercial use. The home page says commercial use requires a paid plan.

## Questions people ask

### Is IPGeolocation.io free?

Yes, for testing and small non-commercial projects. The Free plan gives you 1000 API credits per day and needs no credit card.

### What happens when I go over the IPGeolocation.io free limit?

The free plan stays capped at its daily credits and has no overage billing. The docs name HTTP 429 for paid plans without overage; they do not spell out the free-plan response.

### Can I use the IPGeolocation.io free plan commercially?

No. The home page says commercial use requires a paid plan.
