You set it up in a web console with a code snippet or a plugin, and you send pushes from the dashboard or the REST API. It suits publishers and shop owners without a developer on staff, and developers who prefer an API.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers. The pricing page says all features are included on every plan, and the plans differ only by subscriber count.

That means unlimited websites, push notifications, in-browser messaging, team members, segments, API access, integrations, and custom user attributes. You also get real-time delivery analytics.

A subscriber is a unique user who is actively subscribed and able to receive pushes. People who unsubscribed do not count. The plan has no time limit and needs no credit card or commitment. Webpushr works on both secure and non-secure sites.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page says the free plan stays free as long as your subscriber total is less than 10,000. It does not describe what happens when you cross that line.

Paid plans start at $29 per month for up to 50K subscribers, then $49 for up to 100K and $99 for up to 150K. Above that, you contact the company. The billing cycle is monthly with no minimum term, and you can cancel any time.

## Gotchas

- The 10,000 cap counts active subscribers, so a growing list reaches it quickly even if some people leave.
- The REST API needs a site-specific key and token in the request headers. You find them under Integration, then API Keys.
- The pricing page does not say what happens when you pass the cap, so check your count before a big campaign.
