You set up Realtime Database in the Firebase console and use it through Firebase SDKs that keep a live connection to your data. It suits apps that need simple live syncing.

## What the free plan gives you

On the no-cost Spark plan, you are not billed for Realtime Database. You get 1 GB of data storage and 10 GB per month of data downloads, with no card needed to start. The simultaneous connection limit on Spark is 100. A connection is one mobile device, browser tab, or server app connected to the database.

Downloads count all bytes sent out, including protocol and encryption overhead. Traffic from operations that security rules deny also counts. Reads and writes from the Firebase console count too.

## When you hit a limit

The pages do not say what the app sees when Spark usage runs out, only that you are not billed on Spark. To go past the limits, upgrade the project to the pay-as-you-go Blaze plan. You keep the no-cost 1 GB of storage and 10 GB per month of downloads, and you are billed for use beyond that.

On Blaze, storage costs $5 for each GB per month. Budget alerts warn you about cost, but they do not cap usage or charges.

## Gotchas

- The Spark plan allows only 100 simultaneous connections.
- Downloads include protocol overhead and rejected requests, so small frequent connections cost more than payload size suggests.
- On Blaze, budget alerts do not stop spending.

## Questions people ask

### Is Firebase Realtime Database free?

Yes, on the Spark plan. You get 1 GB of storage and 10 GB per month of downloads with no billing, and up to 100 simultaneous connections.

### What happens when I exceed the Realtime Database free limits?

The pages do not describe the error behavior. To use more, upgrade to Blaze, where you pay for usage beyond the no-cost amounts.

### How many users can use Realtime Database on the free plan?

The limit is 100 simultaneous connections on Spark. A connection is one device, browser tab, or server connected at once.
