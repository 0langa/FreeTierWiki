You use Data Miner as a browser extension. Open a page, pick a recipe that tells it what to grab, and download the result. It suits researchers, sales and recruiting teams, and small businesses.

## What the free plan gives you

The Starter plan costs $0 per month and lets you scrape 500 pages per month. The count resets each month, and unused scrapes do not carry over. It is restricted on some domains.

Recipes tell Data Miner which parts of a page to extract. The Chrome Web Store page says there are over 1 million user-made recipes, and that many popular websites convert to CSV in one click. You can export to XLS, CSV, XLSX, or TSV files, or send data to Google Sheets or Microsoft Excel. Your recipes are private by default.

The company says the data you scrape stays private. Paid plans start at $19.99 per month for 500 pages. They go up to $200 per month for 9,000 pages.

## When you hit a limit

This is a hard stop with a lock. If you go over 500 page scrapes in a month, your account is locked indefinitely. You unlock it by upgrading to a paid plan. The page does not describe a way to unlock it without paying.

You are not billed by surprise. Nothing is charged unless you choose a paid plan. When you do upgrade, the company erases your free-plan scrape count and you start from zero on the paid plan. You do not get 500 free scrapes on top of it.

## Gotchas

- Staying under 500 is the only way to keep the account open. Going over locks it, and the count only resets if you do not exceed the limit in that month.
- Moving to the free plan from a paid plan can mark your private recipes as public.
- Cancelling loses unused credits, and the company does not refund a partial month.

## Questions people ask

### Is Data Miner free?

Yes. The free plan lets you scrape 500 pages per month. Paid plans start at $19.99 per month.

### What happens if I go over 500 scrapes on Data Miner?

Your account is locked indefinitely. You can unlock it by upgrading to any paid plan.

### Is the data I scrape with Data Miner private?

Yes. The company says the data you scrape is always private on free and paid plans. New recipes you save are private by default.
