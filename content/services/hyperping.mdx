You work in the Hyperping dashboard; paid plans also offer its API for scripting monitors. It suits founders who want uptime alerts and a status page in one tool. The docs say a new monitor needs only a name and a URL.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan is for personal projects and trying out Hyperping. It includes 20 monitors with 5-minute checks. The page lists HTTP, port, ping, and keyword checks. You also get 1 server monitoring agent, 1 basic status page, and 1 seat.

The pricing page says no credit card is required. Paid plans start with Essentials at an advertised $24 monthly equivalent, with $290 billed yearly. It adds 50 monitors, 3 browser checks, 5 GB of logs a month, and a status page with a custom domain and 100 subscribers.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens when you try to add a 21st monitor. Higher counts sit on paid plans, but the exact enforcement action is unpublished. Free excludes PagerDuty/On-call and API access according to pricing.

Browser checks are not part of Free. The page lists 3 browser checks starting with the Essentials plan. Logs are in alpha and free until metered billing starts, according to a note on the pricing page.

## Gotchas

- The Free plan checks every 5 minutes, so short outages can pass between checks.
- Browser checks and status page subscribers need a paid plan.
- Free has 1 seat; Essentials includes 3 seats and adds $7 per month per additional user, and Pro includes 10 seats.

## Questions people ask

### Is Hyperping free?

Yes. The Free plan includes 20 monitors with 5-minute checks and 1 basic status page. No credit card is required.

### What does the Hyperping free plan not include?

Browser checks, extra status pages, and subscribers are paid features. The free plan has 1 seat and 1 server monitoring agent.

### Does Hyperping need a credit card?

No. The pricing page and the quickstart both say no credit card is required to create an account.
