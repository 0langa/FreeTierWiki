You use it in a browser: type a URL on the SiteCheck page and submit. There is a version for WordPress sites too. It suits site owners who want an outside view of their site's health.

## What the free plan gives you

SiteCheck is a free website security scanner. You enter a URL, and it checks the site for known malware, viruses, blacklist status, website errors, out-of-date software, and malicious code. It looks at your external source code for malicious links, redirects, iframes, JavaScript, or spam. It also checks if you are on a blocklist kept by authorities such as Google and PhishTank, and it looks for an outdated CMS or vulnerable plugins.

The page lists no price, no account, and no scan quota. It is free for any public URL.

## When you hit a limit

The page does not state a limit on the number of scans. The limit is in what the scanner can see. It visits your site like an everyday user, so it only has access to what is visible in the browser and cannot detect anything on the server side. Sucuri says remote scanners have limited access and results are not guaranteed. For a full scan of every file, you need the paid Sucuri platform. Malware removal is also a paid service.

## Gotchas

- A clean result is not proof of a clean site. Server-side backdoors, phishing pages, and mailers can stay hidden from a remote scan.
- The scanner finds problems but does not fix them. Removal belongs to the paid platform.
- It works on public URLs only.

## Questions people ask

### Is Sucuri SiteCheck free?

Yes. Sucuri describes SiteCheck as a free website security scanner for any public URL.

### Can Sucuri SiteCheck remove malware?

No. It only scans and reports. Sucuri sells malware removal as a separate paid service.

### Why can SiteCheck miss malware on my site?

It scans from the outside and sees only what a browser sees. It cannot see server-side files, so a full file scan needs the paid Sucuri platform.
