You can upload a list in the client area, call the REST API through open source SDKs, or add a JavaScript widget to a form. It suits developers who want to check sign-ups and teams who clean mailing lists.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan issues 25 verification credits per day. One credit buys one standard email verification, so you can check 25 addresses a day. The API page says you can use them to test the service. The free account gets you into the client area, the API, and the bulk list tools, which accept plain text, TSV, CSV, and Excel files.

Free credits expire at the end of each day and do not accumulate. They reset at midnight Greenwich Mean Time. The service also detects free addresses such as Gmail or Yahoo, and it removes duplicates for free.

## When you hit a limit

When the daily credits are gone, you can wait for the reset or buy credits. Purchased credit packs never expire, and the service uses your daily free credits before it uses purchased packs. Higher quality levels cost more per address: High takes 2 credits and Extreme takes 4, compared with 1 for Standard. Paid plans also exist with larger daily free credits. Nothing is charged unless you buy credits or upgrade.

## Gotchas

- You must create an account to verify emails or to buy credits, even for a one-time list.
- Unused free credits expire daily, so a quiet day does not give you extra credits the next day.
- A higher quality level uses more credits per address, so plan your credit use.

## Questions people ask

### Is Verifalia free?

Partly. The Free plan includes 25 email verifications per day. You buy credit packs for more.

### Do unused Verifalia credits roll over?

Free daily credits do not. They expire each day. Credits from purchased packs do not expire.

### Does Verifalia have an API?

Yes. It has a REST API and free open source SDKs for .NET, Java, Node.js, Ruby, PHP, and Go.
