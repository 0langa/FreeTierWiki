You open the site, copy your unique URL, and watch requests arrive in the browser. The CLI forwards them to a local server. It suits developers who build or debug integrations that depend on webhooks.

## What the free plan gives you

UseWebhook is free, and you do not need an account, an API key, or any setup. You can create multiple webhook URLs and send as many requests as you like. The FAQ lists no paid plan, and the pricing page we could read shows none.

Each webhook URL stores the 20 most recent requests. Request bodies are limited to 10 kB. For every request you see the headers, the body, and the metadata. You can forward requests to localhost with the CLI and replay any past request from your history.

The site says all data is encrypted in transit and at rest, and that it keeps data retention low.

## When you hit a limit

The FAQ says there is no cap on how many requests you send, but the site limits some operations to prevent abuse. The pages do not say which operations or what the rate is.

When more than 20 requests arrive for one URL, the oldest ones drop out of the stored list. A request body over 10 kB goes past the stated limit, and the pages do not say what the service does with it. There is no bill, because the service shows no paid plan.

## Gotchas

- Only the 20 most recent requests are kept for each webhook URL, so save any payload you need to keep.
- Request bodies are limited to 10 kB, which excludes large file uploads.
- The service is for testing. The site lists no delivery guarantee for production traffic.

## Questions people ask

### Is UseWebhook free?

Yes. It is free, with no account needed. You can create multiple webhook URLs and send as many requests as you like.

### Do I need to sign up for UseWebhook?

No. The FAQ says you just open the site and start sending requests to your unique URL, with no account, API key, or setup.

### Can UseWebhook forward webhooks to localhost?

Yes. Install the UseWebhook CLI and run it with your URL and the forward-to option pointing at your local endpoint. You can also replay past requests from history.
