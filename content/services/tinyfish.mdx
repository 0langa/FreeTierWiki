TinyFish has endpoints for finding pages and for fetching a page when you have its URL. You can use them together to supply web content to a retrieval workflow. Their billing treatment is separate from TinyFish products that run browser sessions or carry out agent actions.

## What the free plan gives you

TinySearch allows 30 requests per minute and 500 per hour. TinyFetch allows 150 URLs per minute and 1,000 per day. The pricing FAQ says both remain free within those limits even when the Wallet balance is zero. The service advertises a no-card start for the free endpoints. Use search to find a relevant page, then pass the selected URL to Fetch. Keeping the actions separate makes it easier to account for usage. For example, you can fetch several relevant URLs from one query, and those URLs use the Fetch allowance.

## When you hit a limit

Both the short and longer limit windows apply. A workflow can stay under the per-minute search rate and still reach the hourly allowance. Queue requests with the appropriate window in mind and avoid retrying an entire batch when only a few URLs failed. Higher limits and certain other capabilities use the Wallet or paid add-ons, according to the pricing page. If repeated requests target the same document, reuse a recent result where that meets the freshness needs of your application.

## Gotchas

- Check your code’s product endpoint before running it. TinyBrowser and TinyAgent are separate paid usage surfaces.
- A URL-based fetch meter can be used up by batches of URLs even when they are sent together.
- Search and Fetch keep their free allowances when the wallet is empty.

## Questions people ask

### Do I have to fund the TinyFish Wallet?

Search and Fetch do not draw from it and run within their free limits.

### What if I already know the URL?

Use TinyFetch directly and keep its daily and per-minute limits in mind.

### Is TinyBrowser included?

Browser runtime is separately metered. This entry covers only TinySearch and TinyFetch.
