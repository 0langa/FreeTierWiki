You use tldraw.com in the browser. Drawing starts at once on the canvas, and a Share button asks you to sign in. Developers can also build on the tldraw SDK.

## What the free plan gives you

The site calls itself free and instant collaborative whiteboarding. The page lists no paid plan for the whiteboard app itself. You get a canvas with tools for drawing, an eraser, arrows, text, notes, media uploads, shapes, lines, a highlighter, a laser pointer, and frames.

The tldraw SDK is a different product. It lets developers put the canvas inside their own apps. The pricing page for the SDK offers a 100-day free trial with no credit card, a free hobby license, and discounted pricing for early-stage startups.

## When you hit a limit

The whiteboard page lists no limits, quotas, or paid tiers, so it shows nothing that triggers a charge. The page does not say how long boards are stored or how many people can join a shared board.

The limit that matters is for sharing. The Share button shows the hint "Sign in to share". The page does not describe what a signed-in account adds beyond sharing. For the SDK, production use needs a license key, according to the GitHub README.

## Gotchas

- Sharing needs a sign-in, even though drawing does not.
- The SDK is licensed separately. You can use it freely in development, but production use needs a license key.
- The site does not describe how long anonymous drawings are kept, so export or save anything important.

## Questions people ask

### Is tldraw.com free?

Yes. The site describes itself as free and instant collaborative whiteboarding. It lists no paid plan for the whiteboard.

### Does tldraw.com need an account?

You can start drawing without one. The Share button asks you to sign in to share a board.

### Is the tldraw SDK free?

For development, yes. The SDK pricing page offers a 100-day free trial and a free hobby license, and production use needs a license key.
