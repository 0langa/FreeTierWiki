OpenReplay records user sessions and shows them with developer tools data, plus funnels, heatmaps, journey analysis, and co-browsing. You can self-host the open-source edition or let OpenReplay run a managed instance. This entry covers the managed side, which developers can try for free.

## What the free plan gives you

The Dedicated plan has a free trial of 7 days, with no credit card required. You get a fully provisioned instance to evaluate before committing. Dedicated is a dedicated VM in a separate VPC, managed by OpenReplay, with no limits on users, recordings, or retention.

It runs on AWS, Google Cloud, or Microsoft Azure across 50 regions, and it can also run in your own cloud. The plan includes single sign-on, custom data retention, and data residency. The open-source edition is free too, but you host it on your own infrastructure. A usage-based Serverless option, where you pay only for the sessions you record, also appears on the page.

## When you hit a limit

The trial lasts 7 days. The pricing page does not say what happens to the instance or its data if you do not continue after that.

If you continue, Dedicated starts at $199 a month and is billed hourly at the rate of your instance, for example $0.276 an hour. You pay only for the hours it runs, but stopped instances are still billed a small amount based on their disk size. Bring-Your-Own-Cloud is billed at a flat monthly rate.

## Gotchas

- Stopping a Dedicated instance does not stop all charges, because disk storage keeps billing.
- Moving between the open-source edition and Dedicated is not supported directly yet.
- Instance size sets capacity, and canvas recording or native mobile capture needs more of it.

## Questions people ask

### Is OpenReplay free?

The open-source edition is free to self-host. The managed Dedicated plan has a 7-day free trial and then starts at $199 a month.

### Do I need a credit card for the OpenReplay trial?

No. The pricing page says the Dedicated trial needs no credit card.

### Am I billed when my Dedicated instance is stopped?

Yes, a small amount. Stopped instances are still billed for their disk size.
