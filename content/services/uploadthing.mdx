You define a file router on your server and drop an upload button or dropzone into your front end. UploadThing handles the upload itself, and you keep authentication on your own server. It suits TypeScript developers.

## What the free plan gives you

The free 2GB App plan costs $0 per month. It includes 2GB of storage, shared between all your apps, along with unlimited uploads and downloads. Audit log retention is 7 days.

Each upload goes through a file route. A route sets the allowed file types, the maximum file size, and how many files a user can upload. It can also run server code before the upload, such as an auth check, and after the upload finishes.

The paid plans add storage, longer log retention, Regions, and Private Files. Those features are not listed for the free plan.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens when you use all 2GB. It lists storage as the limit and unlimited uploads and downloads, with no bandwidth cap.

The 100GB App plan costs $10 per month and includes 100GB of storage and 30 days of audit logs. The usage-based plan starts at $25 per month with 250GB included, then charges $0.08 per GB above that. No card is needed for the free plan, so the entry records no overage bill.

## Gotchas

- UploadThing runs your upload-complete callback from its own servers. If it cannot reach your app, the callback fails. In production this can happen when your middleware or a firewall blocks the route.
- Keep /api/uploadthing out of your auth middleware. It serves as a validation endpoint and as a webhook.
- Import the package styles file, or the upload components look unstyled.

## Questions people ask

### Is UploadThing free?

Yes. The free 2GB App plan costs $0 per month and includes 2GB of storage, unlimited uploads and downloads, and 7 days of audit logs.

### What does UploadThing cost after the free plan?

The 100GB App plan costs $10 per month. The usage-based plan starts at $25 per month with 250GB included.

### Does UploadThing work with frameworks other than Next.js?

Yes. The docs list guides for Remix, SvelteKit, Astro, Nuxt, SolidStart, Expo, and backend adapters such as Express and Fastify.
