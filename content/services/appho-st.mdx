You use the web dashboard to upload a build and get a share link. A signed-upload API also lets a build pipeline push files from the command line.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan covers 5 apps and 50 downloads per month. Each file can be up to 100 MB. The plan keeps one app version, so you cannot roll back to old builds, and download stats are not included.

Share links do not expire, and one link serves both the iOS and Android version of an app. The same link keeps working when you upload a new version. The site also offers a free IPA and APK inspector, so you can read the metadata inside a package file. Basic is the next step up, with 10 apps and 250 downloads per month.

## When you hit a limit

When you pass the monthly download limit, the dashboard tells you that you are over it. People who open your share links cannot install the app until the limit resets or you move to a paid plan.

Free accounts with more than 90 days of inactivity may have their files removed. You can upload them again, and they use the same link. The site suggests keeping an app downloaded now and then, or going on a paid plan, to avoid this.

## Gotchas

- The Free plan has no download stats, so you cannot see where the 50 downloads went.
- The Free plan keeps only one version of an app, and paid plans keep more.
- The site calls the service a lightweight alternative to MDM for a chosen group of users, not a public store.

## Questions people ask

### Is AppHost free?

Yes. The Free plan allows 5 apps and 50 downloads per month, with files up to 100 MB. Basic starts at $3.49 per month on monthly billing.

### Does AppHost need a credit card?

The site lists no card for the Free plan, and you can sign in with Google or an email address.

### What happens when I go over the AppHost download limit?

Users who open your share links cannot install the app until the limit resets or you upgrade. The dashboard shows a message that you are over your limit.
