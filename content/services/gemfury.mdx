You upload packages in the dashboard, with the `fury` command line tool, or with cURL. Then you install them with your normal package manager through a Repo URL. It suits solo developers and small teams.

## What the free plan gives you

The Public plan costs $0 per month. It gives you unlimited public packages and unlimited collaborators. It has no private packages. All plans come with unlimited versions, SSL protection, and free setup.

If you want private packages, Gemfury offers a 14-day free trial that needs no credit card. During the trial you can use Gemfury without limits. At the end Gemfury tells you which paid plan fits your usage.

The Personal plans start at $9 per month, and the Team plans start at $25 per month. Package files can be up to 300MB. Uploads through a repository endpoint are limited to 20MB.

## When you hit a limit

The trial is the main limit. The pricing page says Gemfury will notify you of the recommended plan after 14 days. It does not say what happens to your private packages if you do not pay.

For file sizes, the Limits page says package files have an absolute limit of 300MB per file. Uploads to a repository endpoint stop at 20MB. Some package managers send files as Base64 text, which makes the payload more than 30% larger and lowers the real limit.

## Gotchas

- The free Public plan has no private packages. You need a paid plan, or the trial, for them.
- Your Repo URL contains a secret token. Anyone with the URL can install your private packages, so keep it secret.
- Git repositories that Gemfury builds have a 500MB size limit, with a 20MB limit for individual files.

## Questions people ask

### Is Gemfury free?

Partly. The Public plan is free for unlimited public packages. Private packages need a paid plan after a 14-day free trial.

### Does Gemfury need a credit card?

No for the trial. The pricing page says the 14-day free trial needs no credit card.

### What happens after the Gemfury trial ends?

Gemfury tells you which paid plan it recommends for your usage. The pricing page does not say what happens to private packages if you do not upgrade.
