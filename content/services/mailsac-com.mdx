You use Mailsac in a browser by picking an address at mailsac.com, or from your tests through its REST API with an API key. It suits developers and testers who need quick inboxes and can live with public mail.

## What the free plan gives you

Any @mailsac.com address works without signup. You make up a name, send mail to it, and read it in the inbox checker. Anyone can read these public inboxes, so use made-up data. The docs say public messages are kept for at most 4 days, with a limit of 6 messages per inbox.

A free account adds an API key, 1 private address, and 1,500 operations a month. An operation is one email received, one API call, or one forward or push such as a webhook or WebSocket. A typical test that receives and reads one email uses about 3 operations. Counts reset on the 1st of each month (UTC).

## When you hit a limit

Mailsac emails you at 80% of your limit and again when you pass it. Service keeps working until 125% of the limit. After that, the API pauses until the 1st of the next month, unless you upgrade. The pricing page does not mention a bill for the free plan, and it asks for no card. Upgrades apply at once. Mail sent to public addresses is also throttled to keep the system available for everyone.

## Gotchas

- Polling the API for new mail spends operations fast. The pricing page suggests webhooks and WebSockets, which use one operation per email.
- Custom domains are a paid feature. The free account gets 1 private address only.
- Some websites block disposable email providers like Mailsac, so sign-up mail may never arrive.

## Questions people ask

### Is Mailsac free?

Yes. Public @mailsac.com inboxes need no signup. A free account adds an API key, 1 private address, and 1,500 operations a month.

### What happens when I go over the Mailsac free limit?

You get warning emails at 80% and when you pass the limit. The service continues until 125%, then pauses until the 1st of next month unless you upgrade.

### Does Mailsac need a credit card?

The pricing page lists no card for the free account. Paid plans are billed by card, invoice, or purchase order.
