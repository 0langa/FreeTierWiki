You sign up in the browser, create a space, and run it with a Scrum or Kanban board and a backlog. The Free plan suits small teams, students, and open source groups that want the real Jira tool at no cost.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan is for small teams of fewer than 10 people who are just getting started. It allows up to 10 users, 2 GB of file storage, and community support. Automation is capped at 150 steps per subscription. Scrum and Kanban boards, a backlog, agile reporting, customizable workflows, and basic roadmaps and dependency management are part of the plan. Jira Service Management and Jira Product Discovery have their own, smaller free limits, such as 3 agents.

Jira Free does not include space roles, advanced permissions, anonymous access, audit logs, or an uptime SLA.

## When you hit a limit

Email is the one limit that is clearly enforced. Jira sends at most 100 emails per day on Free. After that, notifications pause until the next day. A paid plan lifts the limit, but it can take up to 30 days to take effect. The pages do not say what happens when you pass 2 GB of storage or use all 150 automation steps. To add an eleventh user or more storage, you move to the Standard plan.

## Gotchas

- Permissions are not customizable. If your site has always been on Free, everyone with access is an admin for all spaces.
- You cannot change the site URL after you create the site, so pick the name with care.
- Atlassian can deactivate a Free site that stays inactive; opening a board or viewing a work item counts as activity.

## Questions people ask

### Is Jira free?

Yes, for small teams. The Free plan covers up to 10 users, 2 GB of file storage, and community support, and it does not ask for a card.

### What happens when I go over the Jira Free limits?

Jira stops sending notification emails after 100 in one day and resumes the next day. For users and storage, the plans page lists the limits, and the next step up is the Standard plan.

### Can I use Jira Free for an open source project?

Yes, as long as the team stays within 10 users. Because permissions cannot be customized, everyone with access to a site that has always been on Free is an admin for all spaces, which may not suit a public project.
