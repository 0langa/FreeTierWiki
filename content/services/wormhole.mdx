You use Wormhole in a web browser, on desktop or on a phone. A mobile app is in the works, and the site works on iOS and Android in the meantime. It suits anyone who wants a private send without an account.

## What the free plan gives you

You can send up to 10 GB, and Wormhole is free. For files up to 5 GB, Wormhole stores your files on its servers for 24 hours. For files larger than 5 GB, it uses a peer-to-peer transfer that sends your files from your browser directly to the recipient.

Wormhole uses end-to-end encryption. It encrypts files with 128-bit AES-GCM before they leave your browser. The secret key is added to the part of the link after the hash mark, which a browser never sends to the server. So Wormhole says it cannot read your files.

Files are also protected by an extra layer of encryption on its servers.

## When you hit a limit

The limits are the size and the time. Files are permanently deleted from the server after 24 hours. A transfer is capped at 10 GB.

For a file over 5 GB, you must keep the page open until the recipient has downloaded it, because nothing is stored on the server. Wormhole says it plans a Pro plan with larger file limits. The FAQ does not say when. Nothing is billed today.

## Gotchas

- The 24-hour clock matters. The recipient needs to download before the link expires.
- Files over 5 GB depend on your browser staying open, so a closed tab ends the transfer.
- Anyone who has the full share link can decrypt the file, because the key is in the link. Send it only through a channel you trust.

## Questions people ask

### Is Wormhole free?

Yes. You can send files up to 10 GB with no account. Wormhole says it plans a Pro plan later.

### How long does Wormhole keep my files?

For files up to 5 GB, the server keeps them for 24 hours and then deletes them permanently. Larger files go straight to the recipient.

### Is Wormhole secure?

Wormhole encrypts files in your browser with end-to-end encryption, and it says it cannot read them. The key lives in the share link.
