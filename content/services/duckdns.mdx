Duck DNS is a free dynamic DNS service hosted on AWS. It gives you subdomains of duckdns.org and points them at an IP address of your choice, so a server whose address changes keeps an easy name.

## What the free plan gives you

There are no plans: the service is free. The FAQ says the team built a service it wanted for itself and aims to keep it free or as cheap as possible to cover running costs, with support from Patreon and donations. The team is two software engineers.

You update a record with a single HTTP or HTTPS request that carries your domain and your account token. You can comma-separate several domains in one update, and if you leave the ip parameter blank, Duck DNS uses the address the request came from. There is also a short URL form for routers that can only send basic HTTPS GET requests. To use your own domain, you point it at your duckdns.org name with a CNAME.

## When you hit a limit

The fetched pages do not give a number of subdomains per account or a rate limit on updates, and they do not describe what happens if you update very often. The FAQ says Duck DNS will block, as much as it can, anyone who abuses the service, under its Terms of Use.

Automatic detection works for IPv4 only. The API specification says to pass an IPv6 address yourself if you want an AAAA record; only IPv4 autodetection is supported.

## Gotchas

- Your token is the only secret in the update URL, so keep it out of public scripts and recreate it from the Account page if it leaks.
- Reverse lookups of your IP will not return your Duck DNS name; the FAQ says only your ISP can set that.
- You can receive email on a Duck DNS name only if you run your own mail server; the MX record matches your A record.

## Questions people ask

### Is Duck DNS free?

Yes. Duck DNS is a free dynamic DNS service supported by Patreon and donations.

### Can I use my own domain with Duck DNS?

Yes, indirectly. At your domain provider you set a CNAME record that points to your duckdns.org name.

### Does Duck DNS support IPv6?

You can set an IPv6 address, but you have to send it yourself, because Duck DNS cannot detect it automatically.
