GoatCounter is an open-source web analytics platform, offered as a free hosted service supported by donations or as an app you host yourself. It aims to give easy, meaningful, privacy-friendly analytics as an alternative to Google Analytics or Matomo, and it suits developers running personal sites, blogs, and small projects.

## What the free plan gives you

GoatCounter.com is currently free for reasonable public usage. Running your personal website or a small-to-medium business on it is fine, but sending millions of pageviews a day is not. Donations are accepted through GitHub Sponsors.

It is privacy-aware: it does not track users with unique identifiers and does not need a GDPR notice. It identifies unique visits without cookies or persistently stored personal data. The script adds about 3.5KB to your site. You can also use a JavaScript-free tracking pixel, send pageviews from your application's middleware, or import them from server logfiles. JSON and CSV exports and a JSON API are documented in the help pages.

## When you hit a limit

There is no published pageview cap and no paid overage. The FAQ explains why you have to verify your email: if you send many millions of pageviews, the author would rather contact you to discuss options than just shut down the account.

For more serious use, GoatCounter suggests self-hosting, which it says is easy. Nothing on the pages describes billing, so there is no bill to run up on the hosted service.

## Gotchas

- Adblockers block GoatCounter too; the FAQ estimates about a third of pageviews are missed, though it varies by site and audience.
- Statistics update once every 10 seconds, so a brand-new visit can take a moment to show.
- Use a real email address at signup, since that is how you are contacted about heavy usage.

## Questions people ask

### Is GoatCounter really free?

Yes, for reasonable public usage. It is donation-supported, and personal sites and small-to-medium businesses are fine.

### Does GoatCounter need a cookie banner?

GoatCounter says it does not track users with unique identifiers and does not need a GDPR notice.

### What if my site gets a lot of traffic?

If you send millions of pageviews, the author would rather contact you to discuss options, and self-hosting is the suggested route for serious use.
