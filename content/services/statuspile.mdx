You use StatusPile in the browser: build a dashboard, pick providers, and read their status in one place. The code is on GitHub under the MIT license if you want to build your own copy. It suits developers and small teams who depend on many SaaS vendors.

## What the free plan gives you

StatusPile has no paid plan. The home page calls it open sourced and free to use. It lists over 30 providers, and you add or remove services in a click. You log in through Auth0 with your favourite platform to save your settings.

The tool collects the public status pages of other providers. It does not run checks on your own sites, so use a monitor for that. The makers built it for their own client work because they could not find a dashboard like it, and they released it for anyone to use or fork.

## When you hit a limit

The home page and the GitHub readme state no usage limit, and they list no paid tier. Nothing there suggests a bill, and the pages name no card or account fee.

The real limit is coverage. You can only add services that StatusPile already supports, and the authors invite you to request new ones or to fork the project and submit a pull request. If the site ever goes away, the open source code lets you run your own copy.

## Gotchas

- The tool shows provider status only. It does not watch your own websites or send alerts that you configure.
- Saving your dashboard needs a login through Auth0.
- The provider list is finite: the page says over 30 providers are available, and you ask for more.

## Questions people ask

### Is StatusPile free?

Yes. The home page says it is open sourced and free to use, and the source code is on GitHub under the MIT license.

### Does StatusPile monitor my website?

No. It shows the public status pages of your providers on one screen. It does not run checks on your own sites.

### Can I add my own provider to StatusPile?

You can request one. The authors ask people to request new services or fork the project and submit a pull request.
