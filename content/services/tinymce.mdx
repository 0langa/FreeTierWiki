You add a script tag with your API key to a page, then start the editor on a text box with a few lines of JavaScript. You count usage in editor loads, not users. It suits developers who want a quick editor.

## What the free plan gives you

The free plan costs $0 and gives you a cloud-hosted version of the open source editor with core editing features. It includes 1,000 editor loads per month and one domain. It also includes a 14-day free trial of advanced features. An editor load happens each time an editor instance starts and fires its init event. If a page has ten editors, one refresh counts as ten loads. The Essential plan includes 5,000 loads and costs $79 per month when paid annually. The Professional plan includes 20,000 loads.

## When you hit a limit

The free plan is the only plan that goes read-only when it reaches the limit. It stays read-only until the end of the month. To avoid this, you can attach a card. Then extra loads cost $40 for every 1,000, rounded up, and the charge comes on your invoice after the billing cycle. Tiny sends email notices at 50%, 70%, and 100% of your loads. The count can take 2 to 4 hours to update. You can check it in the Subscription tab of your account. Without a card, you cannot be billed.

## Gotchas

- Each editor on a page counts as one load, so pages with several editors use up the free loads quickly.
- Until your domain is approved for the key, the editor shows a notice that the domain is not registered. Localhost works without this step.
- If you attach a card to keep the editor working, extra loads bill at $40 per 1,000.

## Questions people ask

### Is TinyMCE free?

Yes. Tiny Cloud has a free plan with 1,000 editor loads per month and one domain. The open source editor can also be self-hosted without editor load limits.

### What happens when I use up my free TinyMCE editor loads?

The editor switches to read-only until the end of the month. You can attach a card to keep it working, and extra loads then cost $40 per 1,000.

### What counts as a TinyMCE editor load?

Each time an editor instance starts and fires its init event, that is one load. Ten editors on one page make ten loads per refresh.
