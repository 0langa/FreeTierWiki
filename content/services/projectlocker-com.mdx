You manage ProjectLocker through its Portal in the browser, and you push code with normal `git` or Subversion tools over SSH. It suits one developer with a small project who wants a hosted repository.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan includes 1 user, 1 project, and 50 MB of storage. All ProjectLocker plans let you pick Subversion or Git for each repository, and they include unmetered bandwidth. The plans page describes the Free plan as the option for people who are just getting started.

The paid plans start with Venture at $19 per month for 5 users, 5 GB, and 5 projects. Equity costs $49 per month for 20 users and 10 GB. Every plan page lists a 15-day free trial with no credit card.

## When you hit a limit

The help pages do not say what happens when you reach the storage quota. They do explain that a repository keeps its history forever, so deleting files in a commit does not shrink it. To reduce the size, ProjectLocker can reset the repository and you import a smaller export, but your history is lost on reset. Free accounts get no downloadable backups. The Free plan asks for no card, so no bill appears.

## Gotchas

- Only account administrators can create projects.
- Free users have no downloadable backups. Dump your Subversion data or use the Trac API if you want to leave.
- You need your SSH public key added under Git Public Keys, and you must be a project member before the key works.

## Questions people ask

### Is ProjectLocker free?

Yes, there is a Free plan with 1 user, 1 project, and 50 MB of storage. The paid plans start at $19 per month.

### Does ProjectLocker need a credit card?

No. The plans page says the 15-day free trial of paid plans needs no credit card, and the Free plan is separate.

### Does ProjectLocker support Git and Subversion?

Yes. You choose Subversion or Git for each repository on every plan.
