You open a connection from the browser or mobile app with a client library, and your server triggers events with a server library or the HTTP API. It suits developers who want a hosted WebSocket service with a short setup.

## What the free plan gives you

The free Sandbox plan allows 200,000 messages per day and 100 concurrent connections. It comes with standard support. A connection is an open client socket; several browser tabs can create several connections. Its channel subscriptions do not increase the count.

A message is anything Pusher receives through its API plus each delivery to a connected user. If you publish one message to 50 subscribers, that counts as 51 messages. The next plan up, Startup, costs $49 per month for 1 million messages per day and 500 connections.

## When you hit a limit

Official support says usage warnings arrive at 80%, followed by a notification when requests stop at the limit. Over-quota event publishes return 403. The connection guide explicitly enforces Sandbox connections immediately; paid connection plans allow headroom to 120%. The general pricing-page headroom wording does not promise Sandbox excess capacity.

You can upgrade at any time from the dashboard, and the price is prorated by day. You can also downgrade or cancel from the same place.

## Gotchas

- The daily message count includes every delivery. A chat room with many users uses messages much faster than the publish count suggests.
- The 100-connection allowance counts open sockets, so multiple tabs from one person can use multiple connections.
- The pricing page says SLAs are available to a select group of enterprise customers, so do not count on one for the Sandbox plan.

## Questions people ask

### Is Pusher free?

Yes. The Sandbox plan is free and includes 200,000 messages per day and 100 concurrent connections.

### What counts as a message on Pusher?

Messages received through the API plus those delivered to connected users. One publish to 50 subscribers counts as 51 messages.

### What happens if I exceed the Pusher free limit?

Support docs say publishes over the event quota return 403, and the service guide says excess Sandbox connections are rejected immediately. Warnings arrive before limits are reached.
