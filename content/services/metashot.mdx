You use Metashot with a single URL: put `https://api.metashot.io/og` with your key and a title into an `og:image` tag, and it returns a 1200 by 630 PNG. There is no SDK to install. It suits bloggers and site owners who want social preview images without a design tool.

## What the free plan gives you

The free plan costs $0 and needs no credit card. It allows 1,000 images per month, 2 API keys, and 3 custom templates. You get seven built-in themes: gradient, dark, light, code, minimal, article, and profile. You can override the background and text colors. The `data_url` option pulls fields from a public JSON endpoint into your image, with a 2 second timeout.

Images render at Cloudflare's edge and are cached for 24 hours, so a repeat request returns the cached PNG in about 50 to 150 ms. A keyless demo endpoint lets you test a design before you sign up, with a watermark.

## When you hit a limit

When you use all 1,000 images in a month, the API returns an HTTP 429 error that says the monthly rate limit was exceeded. The limit resets on the 1st of the next UTC month. Metashot emails you at 80% and again at 100% of your monthly limit. Current usage comes back in the `X-RateLimit-Used` and `X-RateLimit-Remaining` headers. To get more, the Pro plan costs $12 per month for 50,000 images, and Scale costs $49 per month for 250,000.

## Gotchas

- Signed URLs, which stop others from reusing your key or changing your parameters, are only on Pro and Scale.
- Domain restrictions on keys also need Pro. On Free, keep your key out of places where others can copy it.
- Titles are capped at 70 characters and subtitles at 100. The `data_url` option only supports flat JSON objects.

## Questions people ask

### Is Metashot free?

Yes. The free plan allows 1,000 rendered images per month with no credit card. Paid plans start at $12 per month.

### What happens when I go over Metashot's monthly limit?

The API returns a 429 error until the 1st of the next UTC month. You get email warnings at 80% and 100% of the limit.

### Does Metashot need an SDK?

No. You use a plain image URL with query parameters, so it works in any meta tag or framework.
