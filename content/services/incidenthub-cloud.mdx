You use the IncidentHub dashboard to pick the vendors you rely on, then receive alerts in your chat tools. It suits small teams that run on cloud and SaaS services.

## What the free plan gives you

The home page says there is a free tier with 5 monitors, Discord and Slack integration, and no status page. A monitor here is a vendor service you choose to watch, such as a cloud provider or a payment service. The site tracks more than 1125 vendors and adds new ones on request.

For some vendors you can monitor only the components you use, so you get fewer alerts. The docs say notifications arrive within a few seconds of an incident being detected. Paid plans add more monitors, status pages, and extra channels.

## When you hit a limit

At 5 monitors, you cannot add a sixth vendor. You then pick a paid plan. The pricing page does not say what happens if you try to go over on the free plan.

Paid plans start at $35 a month for 50 monitors. Each paid plan offers a 14-day trial, and the page says no credit card is required. The docs say IncidentHub uses Paddle to process payments when you upgrade.

## Gotchas

- IncidentHub watches vendors' own status pages. It does not check your website or API.
- Monitoring all components of a big vendor can produce many alerts, so the docs suggest choosing specific components.
- A status page is not part of the free plan.

## Questions people ask

### Is IncidentHub free?

Yes, there is a free tier. It has 5 monitors, Discord and Slack integration, and no status page.

### Does IncidentHub need a credit card?

No. The pricing page says no credit card is required for the 14-day paid trials, and the free tier needs no payment. You sign up with Google or an email address.

### What does IncidentHub monitor?

It monitors third-party cloud and SaaS vendors, such as Google Cloud Platform, Zoom, PayPal, and Office 365. It does not monitor your own site.
