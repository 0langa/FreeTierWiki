You call Disease.sh as a plain REST API with HTTP GET requests and read JSON back. It suits developers, students, and researchers who want public health numbers in a dashboard or a class project.

## What the free plan gives you

There is only one tier, and it is free. The home page says the services are free to use. You need no account, plan, or API key. You send a GET request and get JSON.

The API gives global COVID-19 overviews, city and region mobility data, and data on the current influenza outbreak. It also includes official government data for some countries. It pulls from public sources such as Worldometers and public time-series data on GitHub, along with several national health sources.

The API is open source under the GPL-3.0 license. A core team of 4 people from 4 different countries runs it. You can also run your own copy. The repository has Docker setup, and the service needs Redis.

## When you hit a limit

The pages do not state any rate limit, quota, or paid plan. There is nothing to be billed for, so the cost risk is none. The pages also give no promise about uptime or speed.

The more useful limit is in the terms. You may not charge users a fee for your application unless you have a separate agreement with NovelCOVID or its written permission. If you need heavy traffic or a paid product, run your own copy or ask the team first.

## Gotchas

- The pages promise no uptime and name no SLA. A core team of 4 people runs the service.
- The data comes from public sources, so numbers can lag or differ from official figures.
- The terms bar you from charging for your app without permission, and you must remove content on request if it infringes someone's rights.

Yes. The home page says its services are free to use. You do not need an account or API key.

The site and repository do not state one. There is also no promise about speed or uptime.

### Can I use Disease.sh in a paid app?
