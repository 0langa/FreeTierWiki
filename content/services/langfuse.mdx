You instrument your app with the Langfuse SDK or an OpenAI wrapper, then explore traces, prompts, and scores in the web app. It suits developers who build LLM apps and want to inspect every call.

## What the free plan gives you

The Hobby plan is free and needs no credit card. It includes all platform features with limits: 50k units per month, 30 days of data access, 2 users, and community support through GitHub. It is described as good for hobby projects and proofs of concept.

A billable unit is any tracing data point you send, including the trace, its observations such as spans, events, and generations, and scores from evaluations. Core costs $29 per month with 100k units included, 90 days of data access, and unlimited users.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens on Hobby after the 50k units are used up. It shows no additional usage price for Hobby, while Core charges $8 per 100k extra units.

The pricing page defines a billable unit as a trace, an observation, or a score, so one request can use several units. Hobby also lists rate limits apart from the monthly units: 1,000 requests per minute for ingestion and 30 requests per minute for the general API.

## Gotchas

- Units count observations and scores, not only traces, so one request can use several units.
- Data older than 30 days is not accessible on Hobby.
- Hobby has 2 users, while Core has unlimited users.

## Questions people ask

### Is Langfuse free?

Yes. The Hobby plan is free with no credit card, and it includes 50k units per month, 30 days of data access, and 2 users. You can also self-host the open-source version.

### What counts as a unit in Langfuse?

A billable unit is any tracing data point: a trace, an observation such as a span, event, or generation, or a score.

### Is Langfuse open source?

Yes. The docs say Langfuse is open source and that you can use Langfuse Cloud or self-host it with the same instrumentation code.
