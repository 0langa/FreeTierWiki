You manage terms and translations in the POEditor web app. You import files, link a code host, or call the API. It suits developers, project owners, and translators who share a project.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan costs $0 per month and asks for no card. It allows 1,000 strings and unlimited localization projects. The pricing FAQ says you can add up to 5 contributors. The API and image support are limited on the Free column of the plan table.

The string count is the sum of terms and translations in all projects on your account or organization. Contributors do not need their own subscription, and their work counts against your plan.

A 10-day trial of paid features starts when you create your first project, and it needs no card. After it, you can pay or stay on the Free plan. Software with an OSI-approved license can use POEditor free. You create a project and fill in the Open Source Project Request in its settings.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens when a Free account goes over 1,000 strings. It says you can upgrade at any time, and that you pay only the difference between the two plans for the rest of the period.

The paid plans start at $20 per month for Start, with 3,000 strings. Plus has 10,000 strings for $60 per month. Billing every 6 months or yearly lowers these prices.

## Gotchas

- Strings count terms and translations together. A term with several languages uses several strings.
- The Free plan has a cap of 5 contributors, even though the table row says unlimited contributors with an asterisk.
- The trial of paid features lasts 10 days. Features such as the API and images are limited after it ends.

## Questions people ask

### Is POEditor free?

Yes. The Free plan allows 1,000 strings and unlimited projects, with up to 5 contributors. It needs no credit card.

### Does POEditor need a credit card?

No. The pricing FAQ says POEditor asks for no payment details until you switch to a paid plan.

### Is POEditor free for open source?

Software with an OSI-approved license can use POEditor free. You send an Open Source Project Request from the project settings.
