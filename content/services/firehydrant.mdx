FireHydrant is an incident management platform. It runs incidents through a chatbot in Slack or Microsoft Teams, automates repeat steps with runbooks, publishes status pages, and offers on-call and alerting through a product called Signals. It suits teams that want a defined incident process.

## What the free plan gives you

The pricing page lists a Free plan, "perfect for teams who want to poke around FireHydrant." It includes up to 10 responders, 2 runbooks, a Slack and Teams chatbot, 1 public status page, and 3 integrations.

The paid Pro plan costs $25 per responder per month billed annually. It adds unlimited public status pages, a service catalog, on-call scheduling and escalation policies, but includes 5 runbooks and 5 integrations. Enterprise adds unlimited runbooks and integrations, plus private incidents. FireHydrant also offers a 14-day free trial of the Pro plan with no credit card required, which gives full access to all features while you decide.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens at Free responder, runbook or integration limits, or whether ongoing Free use needs a card. The FAQ says Signals, the on-call and alerting product, is charged based on the number of alerts you send, and notifications by SMS and voice are available for purchase. Check what is turned on in your account before you route production alerts through it.

## Gotchas

- On-call scheduling and escalation policies are listed under the paid Incident Management plan, not the Free plan.
- Only 3 integrations are included, which a monitoring tool, a chat tool, and a ticket tracker can use up.
- SMS and voice notifications are a separate purchase.
