You use Aionda Mail in the browser. Create an account, make disposable addresses in the mailbox, and read mail there or have it forwarded. It suits people who want spam protection without keeping a second inbox elsewhere.

## What the free plan gives you

The free plan costs 0 euros. The pricing section lists up to 25 disposable addresses, plus a calendar and contacts. Mail to a disposable address lands in your encrypted mailbox or is forwarded automatically.

The site describes the mailbox as end-to-end encrypted and zero-knowledge, with PGP support and webmail. The pricing section does not say which of those extras the free plan includes, so check them on sign-up. The paid Plus plan costs 1.99 euros per month. It lifts the address limit to 10,000 and adds unlimited forwarding, custom domains, calendar invitations, and a 5 GB encrypted drive.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing section does not say what happens after the 25th address. The Plus plan is the listed way to get more addresses. The sign-up steps on the page ask for a username, a password, and a short game check; they do not mention a payment card.

The site also does not state a forwarding cap for free accounts. It lists "unlimited forwarding" only under Plus, which suggests the free plan is limited. The pricing section gives no number.

## Gotchas

- At sign-up you get recovery words. The site says it cannot help you get into the mailbox if you forget your password and lost the words.
- The AI assistant is on by default for new accounts. The site says it runs on its own servers in Germany and can be turned off at any time.
- Trashmail.com now leads to Aionda Mail, so older guides and screenshots may not match.

## Questions people ask

Yes. The free plan costs 0 euros and allows up to 25 disposable addresses, plus a calendar and contacts.

### What is the difference between free and Plus on Aionda Mail?

Plus costs 1.99 euros per month. It allows up to 10,000 disposable addresses and adds unlimited forwarding, custom domains, calendar invitations, and a 5 GB encrypted drive.

The site that used to be Trashmail.com is now Aionda Mail. The page says disposable addresses have existed there since 2002.
