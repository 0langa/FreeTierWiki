Azure Managed Disks are persistent block storage volumes for Azure virtual machines. You provision a disk type and capacity, attach it to a VM, and manage it through the portal, CLI or templates. This entry covers the publicly confirmed introductory Azure credit; it does not promise an ongoing disk-specific free allowance.

## What the free plan gives you

New Azure free-account customers receive $200 of shared credit for 30 days. Microsoft Learn says it can be used on Azure services other than third-party Marketplace purchases. Managed Disks is a first-party Azure service, so disk usage can consume that introductory balance alongside the VM, network and other resources you create. Other services share this balance.

Current public offer pages do not confirm a dedicated ongoing free-disk allocation or its eligible redundancy settings. Check the Free services benefits attached to your subscription before deployment. The introductory credit is shared across services and should not be confused with a permanent disk-specific quota.

Azure offers Ultra Disks, Premium SSD v2, Premium SSD, Standard SSD and Standard HDD. The type, provisioned capacity, performance options and redundancy affect cost. Billing is based on provisioned resources, not just the files stored on them.

## When you hit a limit

If the free-account credit is spent or its 30 days expire, services are disabled unless you move to pay-as-you-go. Unused credit expires at the original deadline. Upgrading earlier keeps the remaining credit for the rest of those 30 days, but enables charges for usage outside applicable credit or confirmed free benefits.

Managed disks are billed by their configured size and type. Snapshots, additional disks and related VM or networking resources can consume credit or incur separate charges. A VM's free compute allowance does not establish free disk eligibility. Monitor the subscription's cost view and remove resources you no longer need.

## Gotchas

- Standard Azure registration requires a phone number and an accepted credit or non-prepaid debit card. A temporary verification hold may appear.
- The $200 belongs to the Azure account, not to each disk or VM. Other services use the same balance.
- Stopping a VM does not remove its persistent disks. Review remaining disks and snapshots as well as compute before ending an experiment.

## Questions people ask

### Is Azure Disk Storage free?

New customers can pay for eligible first-party Azure usage with the shared $200 credit for 30 days. This review does not confirm a dedicated ongoing disk quota.

### Does Azure confirm an ongoing free-disk quota?

No current public offer page reviewed here confirms it. Check your subscription's Free services benefits for exact eligibility before deploying.

### What happens when the introductory credit ends?

The free account's services stop unless you upgrade. Pay-as-you-go enables continued usage and charges beyond remaining credit or confirmed benefits.
