Azure Managed Disks are persistent block storage volumes for Azure virtual machines. You provision a disk type and capacity, attach it to a VM, and manage it through the portal, CLI or templates. This entry covers the 12-month disk allowance for new accounts and the introductory Azure credit.

## What the free plan gives you

New Azure free-account customers receive $200 of shared credit for 30 days. Microsoft Learn says it covers Azure services other than third-party Marketplace purchases, so disk usage can draw on it alongside your VM and network resources.

Microsoft's free-services list also names Managed Disks as a 12-month benefit for new customers: two 64 GB (P6) solid-state drives, plus a 1 GB snapshot and 2 million I/O operations. Only the en-in version of the page shows its table without scripts. It does not state the redundancy setting, so check the Free services benefits on your subscription before deployment.

Azure offers Ultra Disks, Premium SSD v2, Premium SSD, Standard SSD and Standard HDD. Type, provisioned size, performance options and redundancy affect cost, and billing follows what you provision, not what you store.

## When you hit a limit

If the free-account credit is spent or its 30 days expire, services are disabled unless you move to pay-as-you-go. Unused credit expires at the original deadline. Upgrading earlier keeps the remaining credit for the rest of those 30 days, but enables charges for usage outside applicable credit or listed free amounts. The free disk amounts last 12 months, and disks you keep after that are billed at normal rates.

Managed disks are billed by their configured size and type. Snapshots, additional disks and related VM or networking resources can consume credit or incur separate charges. A VM's free compute allowance does not establish free disk eligibility. Monitor the subscription's cost view and remove resources you no longer need.

## Gotchas

- Standard Azure registration requires a phone number and an accepted credit or non-prepaid debit card. A temporary verification hold may appear.
- The $200 belongs to the Azure account, not to each disk or VM, and other services use the same balance. Only the listed disks are free; a larger or third disk is billed or uses credit.
- Stopping a VM does not remove its persistent disks. Review remaining disks and snapshots as well as compute before ending an experiment.

## Questions people ask

### Is Azure Disk Storage free?

New customers get two 64 GB (P6) SSD managed disks, a 1 GB snapshot and 2 million I/O operations free for 12 months, according to Microsoft's free-services list. The shared $200 credit for 30 days can also pay for disk usage.

### How long do the free disks last?

Twelve months, and only for new customers. The list does not state the redundancy setting, so check your subscription's Free services benefits for exact eligibility before deploying.

### What happens when the introductory credit ends?

The free account's services stop unless you upgrade. Pay-as-you-go enables continued usage and charges beyond remaining credit or confirmed benefits.
