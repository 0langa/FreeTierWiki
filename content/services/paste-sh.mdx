You use Paste.sh in a browser by typing into a text area, or from a shell with a small script that sends a file or command output. It suits developers who want a quick link to share text without making an account.

## What the free plan gives you

Paste.sh is free to use and needs no account. The page lists no price, no plan, and no sign-up. The web page gives you a text area and saves as you type. JavaScript in your browser encrypts the text with AES-256 before it goes to the server. The key sits in the part of the URL after the `#`, which the browser never sends to the server, so the server cannot read your paste.

There is a limit of 1 MB of data per paste once encrypted. The command line client can also make public pastes with a `-p` option. Those are not encrypted, but they have shorter URLs.

## When you hit a limit

The page states no quota, no retention period, and no service guarantee, so the only stated limit is 1 MB per paste. For larger files, the site tells you to use a file sharing site instead. Because there is no card or plan, no bill can appear. The site cannot scan or remove most content, because it is encrypted. It asks you to report abuse, and it does not allow illegal or abusive content.

## Gotchas

- Share the whole URL. If you drop the part after the `#`, the paste cannot be decrypted. Other systems may also store that part, depending on how you share the link.
- The site keeps the time of each paste, your IP address, your User-Agent, and a cookie that lets you edit the paste later. Use the command line client to avoid the cookie.
- The web version needs JavaScript, browser crypto support, and cookies.

### Is Paste.sh free?

Yes. The site lists no price or plan, and you do not need an account.

### How big can a Paste.sh paste be?

The about page sets a limit of 1 MB of data per paste once it is encrypted.

### Does Paste.sh delete my paste?

The page does not say. It states no retention period or service guarantee.
