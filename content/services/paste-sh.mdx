You use Paste.sh in a browser by typing into a text area, or from a shell with a small shell script. It suits developers who want a quick encrypted link to share text.

## What the free plan gives you

Paste.sh lists no price, no plan, and no sign-up on its about page. The web page gives you a text area. JavaScript in your browser encrypts the text with AES-256 before it goes to the server. The key sits in the part of the URL after the `#`, which the browser never sends to the server, so the server cannot read your paste.

There is a limit of 1 MB of data per paste once encrypted. The command line client can also make public pastes with a `-p` option. Those pastes are not encrypted.

## When you hit a limit

The page states no quota, no retention period, and no service guarantee, so the only stated limit is 1 MB per paste. The page does not say what happens with a larger paste. Because there is no card or plan, no bill can appear. The site cannot scan or remove most content, because it is encrypted. It asks you to report abuse, and it does not allow illegal or abusive content.

## Gotchas

- Share the whole URL. If you drop the part after the `#`, the paste cannot be decrypted. Other systems may also store that part, depending on how you share the link.
- The site keeps the time of each paste, your IP address, your User-Agent, and a cookie that lets you edit the paste later. Use the command line client to avoid the cookie.
- The web version needs JavaScript, browser crypto support, and cookies.
