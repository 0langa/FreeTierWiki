You create a Translator resource in the Azure portal, then send text to the REST API with a key or through an SDK. It suits developers who localize apps, sites, and support content.

## What the free plan gives you

The F0 tier includes 2 million characters per month. The pricing page says this pool is shared between standard translation and custom translation training. Text translation and language detection are part of the standard features.

The service also sets a rate limit. On F0 you can send 2 million characters per hour, and the docs say to spread them evenly, at roughly 33,300 characters per minute. Each translate request can hold up to 50,000 characters, counted across all target languages. A 3,000-character text sent to three languages counts as 9,000. There is no limit on concurrent requests.

Document translation is a paid feature. It is not part of the free tier.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens after the 2 million monthly characters. The docs describe the hourly limit: if you pass it, or send too much too fast, you get an out-of-quota response.

For more room, move to a paid tier. The S1 tier allows 40 million characters per hour. Paid prices appear as blank dashes on the pricing page, so use the Azure pricing calculator for real numbers.

## Gotchas

- Characters are counted across every target language, so translating into three languages triples the count.
- Custom translation training uses the same free pool as standard translation.
- A multi-service or regional resource needs two headers, the key and the region. A single-service global resource needs only the key.

## Questions people ask

### Is Azure AI Translator free?

Yes, within limits. The F0 tier gives 2 million characters per month, shared between standard translation and custom translation training.

### Does Azure Translator free tier include document translation?

No. Document translation is a paid feature.

### How fast can I send text on the Translator free tier?

The F0 limit is 2 million characters per hour. Spread the characters evenly instead of sending them in a burst.
