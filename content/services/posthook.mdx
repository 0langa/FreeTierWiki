You schedule a hook with one API call, and Posthook sends a signed HTTP request to your endpoint at the time you choose. You can also test locally with the Posthook listener. It suits backend developers who want delayed work without a job queue.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan costs $0 and includes 1,000 hooks a month, 3 projects, and email alerts. The site says it is for validating a scheduling pattern end to end before production. It needs no credit card, and it replaces a time-limited trial, so you can use it as long as you need.

Only the first scheduling counts toward the quota. Deliveries, retries, and other API calls do not count. A 5-email drip campaign for one user counts as 5 hooks. The count resets on the first of each month, and unused hooks do not roll over. Every hook is kept and can be searched for up to 90 days.

Paid plans start with Launch at $39 a month for 20,000 hooks and 5 projects.

## When you hit a limit

You get a heads-up email at 75% of your quota and another notice at 100%. Scheduling keeps working, and you can use up to 150% of your quota. Past 150%, new hooks are rejected until the first of the next month.

The pricing page says there are no surprise bills. You can check your usage on the Account Page, and each POST to the hooks endpoint returns quota headers.

## Gotchas

- Past 150% of your quota, new hooks are rejected until the next month starts.
- Annual billing is not offered at this time.
- Delivery is at least once, so your endpoint should handle a repeated delivery of the same hook.

## Questions people ask

### Is Posthook free?

Yes. The Free plan includes 1,000 hooks a month and 3 projects, with email alerts, and needs no credit card.

### What happens when I hit the Posthook free limit?

You get emails at 75% and 100%, and scheduling keeps working up to 150% of your quota. Beyond that, new hooks are rejected until the 1st of the next month.

### Does Posthook count retries toward my quota?

No. Only the initial scheduling of a hook counts. Deliveries, retries, and API calls are free.
