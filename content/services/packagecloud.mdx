Packagecloud hosts package repositories, so users can install your software with apt, yum, npm, pip, Maven, gem, or Helm instead of downloading files by hand. It supports Debian, RPM, npm, Maven, Python, RubyGems, and Helm repositories. It suits projects that ship Linux packages or libraries to other developers.

## What the free plan gives you

The pricing FAQ says Packagecloud offers a limited Free Plan, and the plan card lists 10 GB of bandwidth, 2 GB of storage, and unlimited CI/CD minutes. The comparison table shows unlimited users and unlimited repositories on every plan, and marks public repositories as included on Free while private repositories and API access are not.

Uploads to Packagecloud are free; bandwidth counts only bytes sent out for downloads, and conditional GETs, HEAD requests, and cancelled requests are not counted. Bandwidth usage is updated every 5 minutes, and you can see current and past usage in account settings.

## When you hit a limit

You get notifications at 50%, 90%, and 99% of your bandwidth, storage, or maximum overage amount. On paid plans, overage is added to the next month's bill, and you can set a maximum overage amount; past it, the account gets disabled until a new billing period, an upgrade, or a higher maximum. The pricing page does not say whether this overage billing applies to the Free plan.

Starter is $89 a month with 20 GB of bandwidth and 5 GB of storage, and every paid plan has a 14 day trial.

## Gotchas

- Storage is a high watermark: if you upload 100GB and delete 90GB, you still used 100GB for that billing period.
- Free repositories are public, so do not push internal builds to them.
- The jump from Free to Starter is $89 a month, a big step for a hobby project.

## Questions people ask

### Is Packagecloud free?

Yes, with a limited Free Plan: 10 GB of bandwidth and 2 GB of storage for public repositories.

### Can I host private packages on the free plan?

No. The comparison table marks private repositories as not included on Free; they start with Starter.

### Do uploads count toward bandwidth?

No. Uploads are free, and only outgoing download bytes count.
