No-IP is a dynamic DNS company. It points an easy hostname at a home or office connection whose IP address changes, and sells larger plans for remote access, managed DNS, and white-label use.

## What the free plan gives you

Free Dynamic DNS gives you 1 hostname and 1 update client. You pick the hostname on one of 30 No-IP owned domains, and the Dynamic Update Client keeps it pointed at your current IP. The plan includes basic DNS records, rapid propagation, the anycast DNS network, and web support. The page says you can start without a credit card.

The plan is aimed at basic remote access: reaching a computer, DVR, webcam, or other device at home by name. It does not include an SSL certificate, and the free plan's feature list does not include a REST API; No-IP lists its RESTful API on higher managed DNS plans.

## When you hit a limit

The main condition is time, not traffic: you must confirm the hostname every 30 days. The paid plans advertise that hostnames never expire, and Personal Remote Access says you never worry about confirming as long as the subscription stays active.

The free plan is limited to 1 hostname, so a second device needs a paid plan. Enhanced Dynamic DNS starts at $2.50 a month for 3 hostnames and offers 80+ No-IP owned domains, with options for 5 or 25 hostnames.

## Gotchas

- If you miss the 30-day confirmation, the paid-plan wording implies the free hostname can expire; set a reminder.
- Only 1 update client is included, so run it on one always-on machine.
- The free plan's feature list has no SSL certificate, so plan your own certificate if you serve HTTPS on the hostname.

## Questions people ask

### Is No-IP free?

Yes. Free Dynamic DNS gives you 1 hostname with no credit card required.

### Why do I have to confirm my No-IP hostname?

The free plan requires you to confirm your hostname every 30 days. Paid plans say hostnames never expire.

### How many hostnames does the No-IP free plan include?

One. The free plan is limited to 1 hostname and 1 update client.
