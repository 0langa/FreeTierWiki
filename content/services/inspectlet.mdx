Inspectlet records how visitors use your website and lets you watch each session as a replay. It also offers heatmaps, A/B testing, form analytics, and JavaScript error capture, so it suits developers who want to see the steps behind a bug report.

## What the free plan gives you

The free plan gives you 2,500 recorded sessions per month across up to 3 websites. Inspectlet says it needs no credit card and has no time limit. It includes session replay, heatmaps, and A/B testing, and you can upgrade when you need more volume or features.

A recorded session is one visitor's entire browsing experience on your site, from the moment they land to when they leave, and every page they visit is captured in that single recording. Data retention depends on the plan, from 1 month on the free plan up to 24 months on Enterprise. You can download individual recordings to keep them permanently.

## When you hit a limit

Inspectlet stops recording new sessions once you reach your monthly limit. Your existing recordings and heatmaps remain accessible.

The limit resets at the start of each billing cycle, or you can upgrade to a higher plan for more capacity. Because the free plan needs no card and stops recording at the cap, it cannot run up a bill. There are no long-term contracts on paid plans, and downgrades take effect at the end of the billing period.

## Gotchas

- Visits after the monthly cap are not recorded, so late-month problems can go unseen.
- Free data is kept for 1 month, so download any recording you need to keep.
- The 2,500 sessions are shared across all 3 websites.

## Questions people ask

### Is Inspectlet free?

Yes. The free plan gives 2,500 recorded sessions per month across up to 3 websites, with no time limit.

### What happens when I reach the session limit?

Inspectlet stops recording new sessions until the next billing cycle, and your existing recordings stay available.

### Does Inspectlet work with single-page apps?

Yes. Inspectlet says it handles React, Angular, Vue, and other JavaScript-heavy frameworks, including client-side navigation.
