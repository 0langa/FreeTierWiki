You install Kreya on your computer, point it at an API, and send requests from a list of operations. It suits solo developers who work with gRPC or several protocols.

## What the free plan gives you

The pricing page calls it a free forever plan for individuals. It covers the core protocols: gRPC, REST, GraphQL, WebSockets, and server-sent events, with HTTP/2 and HTTP/3. Environments and templating are in the core feature list. The download page says Kreya is free to download, with no credit card or login.

The Free plan keeps only the latest request in history. Support is via GitHub. The pages publish no usage caps for the Free plan. Kreya is not open source, according to its FAQ.

## When you hit a limit

The pages publish no usage quota for the Free plan. The limits are features. Scripting, tests, collections, snapshot testing, and timing details are in the Pro and Enterprise highlights, not in Free.

Pro support comes by email with a maximum reaction time of 48 hours on business days. Paid plans have a 10-day trial and can be cancelled anytime. The pricing page does not show dollar prices in the page text.

## Gotchas

- The Free plan keeps only the latest request in history, so older calls are not saved.
- For gRPC you must add protobuf definitions, or Kreya cannot know the services and message types.
- HTTP/3 needs the native `libmsquic` library installed, or requests fail with an error.

## Questions people ask

### Is Kreya free?

Yes. The pricing page lists a free forever plan for individuals that covers gRPC, REST, GraphQL, and WebSockets. Scripting, tests, and collections need a paid plan.

### Does Kreya need a credit card?

No. The download page says no credit card or login is required to download Kreya. Paid plans have a 10-day trial.

### Is Kreya open source?

No. The FAQ says Kreya is not open source.
