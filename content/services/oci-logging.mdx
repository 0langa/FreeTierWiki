OCI Logging is a place to collect and search messages from Oracle Cloud services and applications. Put related logs in a group, then narrow a search by source and event details. This can help when you are tracing what happened around a network or application problem.

## What the free plan gives you

Oracle’s Always Free guide lists up to 10 GB a month across OCI Logging for Free Tier tenancies without active trial credits. The same pool applies across the service rather than supplying a new allowance to each log group. The guide discusses the allowance alongside VCN flow logs, which record network traffic decisions. Depending on the source you enable, Logging can also work with service logs or with logs your application provides. Start with the sources you expect to search. A permissive collection rule can produce far more data than you need to investigate a problem.

## When you hit a limit

Inspect usage at the tenancy level to understand what contributes to the monthly allowance. Oracle’s price list has a paid storage meter above the included amount. The Always Free summary does not describe an automatic spending stop for every account configuration. If logs leave Logging through another Oracle service, check that service’s quotas separately. Plan retention and export with the sensitivity and volume of the data in mind.

## Gotchas

- Enabling flow logs for busy network paths can quickly increase ingestion.
- The free allowance is shared even if you organize workloads into different log groups.
- The Oracle Cloud account requires a card for signup and can place a temporary hold. Check the tenancy type before interpreting a price list.

## Questions people ask

### Can I search logs from the Console?

Yes. Logging provides search across the logs that your account has permission to view.

### Do I get 10 GB for each log group?

The Always Free guide describes 10 GB shared across OCI Logging for eligible Free Tier tenancies.

### Can I send log data elsewhere?

OCI has ways to route log data to other destinations. Check the separate service and destination limits for the route you configure.
