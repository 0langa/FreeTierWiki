Screenshotlayer is an apilayer API that captures a website as an image. You send a request with your access key and a URL, and the snapshot is rendered and stored on a CDN for retrieval. Paid plans add S3 and FTP export and more dedicated workers for concurrent captures.

## What the free plan gives you

The product page lists a Free Plan with 100 snapshots and a Get Free API Key button. The FAQ explains the difference between free and paid: the Free Plan is set out to be a trial version without a time limit, offering a volume of 100 monthly snapshots and a limited set of features. The Basic Plan starts at 10,000 snapshots.

Paid plans, by the same FAQ, add much higher request volumes, secure datastreams through 256-bit HTTPS encryption, unlimited and prioritised technical support, and more API features. Read that as: the free key is for trying the API, not for a live product.

## When you hit a limit

Screenshotlayer notifies you by email and on your account dashboard at 75%, 90%, and 100% of the monthly API request allowance. The FAQ then says overage fees apply after 100% so your application keeps running. That answer is written for subscriptions in general; the product page does not say how it applies to the Free Plan, so act on the 75% and 90% alerts.

## Gotchas

- The vendor itself calls the Free Plan a trial version, so expect missing features rather than a cut-down production tier.
- HTTPS is listed as a paid feature, so calling the API from a secure page or server on the free key may not be possible.
- Usage statistics and low-balance alerts live on the account dashboard; check them before running a batch.

## Questions people ask

### Is Screenshotlayer free?

There is a Free Plan with 100 snapshots a month and no time limit. Screenshotlayer describes it as a trial version with a limited feature set.

### What happens when I pass 100 snapshots?

You get notifications at 75%, 90%, and 100%. The FAQ says overage fees apply past 100% for subscriptions, but it does not spell out the Free Plan's behaviour.

### Does the free plan support HTTPS?

The product page lists 256-bit HTTPS encryption as a feature of paid plans.
