You install the Mergify GitHub App, choose which products to turn on per repository, and then drive the queue with pull request comments such as `@mergifyio queue`. It suits maintainers and small teams that use GitHub.

## What the free plan gives you

Mergify is free for open source projects, with unlimited users. Private teams get the Free plan for up to 5 users. Both plans include all three products: CI Insights, Test Engine, and Merge Queue. The pricing page says the Open Source and Free plans do not cover the same features, so check its table.

CI Insights data retention is 24 hours on the Free and Open Source plans; Test Engine data is kept for 7 days. The paid Max plan keeps data for 30 days. Billing counts only active contributors. A contributor is active for 30 days after opening or updating a pull request. The billing docs include updates to its head branch and count the person who performed the update; bots and inactive teammates do not count.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens when a private team grows past 5 active contributors. The next step is the Max plan, which costs $21 per seat per month and covers up to 100 users. Mergify also offers a free 14-day trial of Max with no credit card. The pages also do not separately state whether permanent Free requires a card.

## Gotchas

- Adding a repository needs GitHub admin rights, and each product is switched on per repository.
- Free CI Insights retention is 24 hours, while Test Engine retention is 7 days. Max keeps both for 30 days.
- Mergify works with GitHub. The docs describe installing a GitHub App and do not mention other hosts.

## Questions people ask

### Is Mergify free?

Yes. It is free for open source projects and for private teams of up to 5 active contributors. All three products are included.

### Does Mergify need a credit card?

The 14-day Max trial needs no card. The reviewed pages do not separately spell out a card requirement for the permanent Free plan.

### Who counts as an active contributor on Mergify?

A real user who opened or updated a pull request in the last 30 days. Updates to its head branch count, while bots and inactive teammates do not.
