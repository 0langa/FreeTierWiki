Gumlet is a media platform with two products: video hosting and streaming, and an image CDN that resizes, compresses, and converts images on the fly. Each product has its own plans on the pricing page.

## What the free plan gives you

The video Free plan is listed as Free Forever with 1 seat, 60 storage minutes, 100 GB of bandwidth a month, and 60 minutes of live streaming a month. It includes 4K and HDR10 support, an ad free player, password protection and geo-blocking, and AI-generated subtitles.

The image Free plan, also Free Forever, includes 30 GB of image delivery bandwidth a month, detailed usage analytics, and GIF and PDF support. The public plan card does not specify a processing request, source count, or image dimension limit.

## When you hit a limit

The image Free plan bills extra bandwidth at $0.50 per GB.

Video Free requires an upgrade for more storage. Gumlet issues a provisional invoice after you exceed the streaming limit. Upgrading before the billing cycle ends voids it. Gumlet does not specify how it handles accounts without a payment method. For accounts created on or after September 1, 2026, Free video includes 1,000 AI credits a month, and extra AI credits are billed at $1 per 1,000. Subtitle generation uses those credits. The Creator plan costs $6 a month billed annually.

## Gotchas

- API and MCP access appear among the Creator plan's additions, so the free video plan is meant for the dashboard and player.
- Free video keeps Gumlet branding; removing it is a Creator feature.
- Storage is counted in minutes of video, not gigabytes, so 60 storage minutes is about an hour of uploads in total.

## Questions people ask

### Is Gumlet free?

Yes. Both the video and image products have Free plans listed as Free Forever, with 100 GB and 30 GB of monthly bandwidth respectively.

### Does the free plan include the video API?

The pricing page lists API and MCP access among the Creator plan's features, not the Free plan's.

### What happens if I go over the free image bandwidth?

The image plan lists overage at $0.5 per GB on the Free plan, and the pricing FAQ says usage beyond your plan is billed at the overage price. The page does not describe a hard stop.
