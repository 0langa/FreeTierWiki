Cloud Scheduler is a managed cron job scheduler for task automation on Google Cloud. A job defines a single activity that runs at the frequency you set, and Google keeps the schedule running for you. It suits developers who need a few timed tasks and do not want a server that exists only to run cron.

## What the free plan gives you

Cloud Scheduler pricing is based only on jobs. Each Google billing account gets 3 jobs per month free, and the actual runs of a job, called executions, are not billed at all. A job that runs every minute costs the same as a job that runs once a day.

The free tier is measured at the account level, not the project level. If your account has 5 projects with 2 jobs each, you have 3 free jobs and 7 paid jobs. The pricing table expresses the free amount as 93 job-days per month, which is three jobs kept for 31 days.

## When you hit a limit

Nothing stops your jobs when you create a fourth one. Each job past the free three is billed $0.10 per 31 days, which is $0.003 per day for each billable job.

Billed usage is calculated per day. If you define a job and delete it 10 days later, Google bills $0.1/31 times 10, which is $0.03. A paused job is still counted as a job, so pausing does not stop the charge; deleting the job does.

## Gotchas

- The 3 free jobs are shared by every project on the billing account, so jobs in a second project use the same allowance.
- A paused job counts as a job and is billed past the free three.
- A job is billed for each day it exists past the free three, whether it runs that day or not.

## Questions people ask

### Is Google Cloud Scheduler free?

For up to 3 jobs per billing account each month. Each job past that costs $0.10 per 31 days.

### Does Cloud Scheduler charge per run?

No. Executions are not billed, so you pay for jobs, not for how often they run.

### Does pausing a job stop the charge?

No. A paused job is counted as a job, so delete jobs you no longer need.
