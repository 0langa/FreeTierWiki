PhantomJsCloud runs a headless browser for you behind an HTTP API. You send a page request and get back a screenshot, a PDF, the rendered HTML, or other output, without hosting a browser yourself. It suits scripts and small services that need pages rendered with JavaScript.

## What the free plan gives you

The pricing page lists The Free Plan at $0 a month with about 500 pages a day. The docs say the same: create a free account to get 500 Pages/Day at the dashboard. The page count is an estimate. Each request uses browser time and data out, and the estimate assumes 2 seconds of browser time and 50KB of data out per page. Pages that take longer or return more data give you fewer pages a day; lighter pages give you more.

Paid subscriptions bill browser time at $0.15 an hour and egress at $0.25 per GB, about $0.000095 a page. For bursts you can buy Prepaid Credit in $20 steps, which the page says never expires.

## When you hit a limit

The free amount is a daily credit. When your account is out of credits, the API returns HTTP 402 Payment Required, and the docs tell you to upgrade your subscription or add prepaid credits. A test key, ak-test-key-no-credits, lets you see that 402 response before it happens for real. Prepaid Credit, if you buy some, is used when you exceed your daily subscription credit.

## Gotchas

- The 500 figure is an estimate; a slow single-page app can use several times the assumed 2 seconds of browser time.
- The demo key in the docs examples is limited to 100 requests per day, so switch to your own key early.
- The official Node.js client handles rate limiting and autoscaling for you; other clients must back off on their own.

## Questions people ask

### Is PhantomJsCloud free?

Yes. The Free Plan is $0 a month and gives about 500 pages a day.

### What happens when the daily credit runs out?

The API returns 402 Payment Required until you have credit again, from the next day's allowance, an upgrade, or prepaid credit.

### Why do I get fewer than 500 pages a day?

The figure assumes 2 seconds of browser time and 50KB of data out per page. Heavier pages use more of the credit each.
