You use UptimeToolbox in a web dashboard, with an Intro Tour for new users. A small Linux agent reports server metrics. It suits small sites and projects that need a status page and a few checks.

## What the free plan gives you

The Basic plan costs $0 per month. The home page lists 5 monitors with 3-minute checks, 1 status page, and 1 server. Data is kept as 7 days of hi-res data and 3 months of historical data. Custom domains are listed on every plan, including Basic.

The site says it also watches your domain expiry and SSL certificates. The Professional plan costs $9 per month and has 25 monitors, 1-minute checks, 3 status pages, and 10 servers. The Business plan costs $29 per month with 100 monitors and 30-second checks.

## When you hit a limit

The home page does not say what happens when you reach 5 monitors, and it does not describe any charge on Basic. Higher counts need a paid plan.

The pricing list shows SMS and voice credit under Basic, but the changelog says that since February 2022 the Pro plan is required for SMS and voice alerts because of abuse. Do not count on phone alerts on the free plan. A 14-day free trial of paid plans needs no credit card.

## Gotchas

- Basic has 5 monitors and 1 server, so a second server needs a paid plan or a custom plan from support.
- SMS and voice alerts need the Pro plan according to the changelog, despite the pricing list.
- The Linux agent needs no extra software and uses tools built into most Linux distributions, such as awk, curl, and sed.

## Questions people ask

### Is UptimeToolbox free?

Yes. The Basic plan is free and lists 5 monitors, 3-minute checks, 1 status page, and 1 server.

### Does UptimeToolbox need a credit card?

No. The site says you can sign up for a 14-day free trial and no credit card is required.

### Does UptimeToolbox send SMS alerts for free?

Probably not. The changelog says the Pro plan is required for SMS and voice alerts, even though the pricing list shows SMS and voice credit under Basic.
