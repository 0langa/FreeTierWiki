You use the Ploi dashboard, an API, or a mobile app to manage servers that you rent from a cloud provider. It suits developers who want Laravel or PHP deploys without writing server scripts.

## What the free plan gives you

The Free plan costs $0. It covers up to 1 server and up to 1 site, with 5 site deployments per month. Paid plans start at $10 per month for Basic, which allows up to 5 servers and unlimited sites and deployments.

Ploi does not host anything for you. The pricing page says its price does not include server fees, so you also pay your cloud provider for the server. The docs list UpCloud, DigitalOcean, Vultr, Linode, Hetzner, AWS, Katapult, and Hostinger as server providers you can connect. Ploi also lists one-click installs for WordPress, Statamic, Craft CMS, Nextcloud, and phpMyAdmin.

## When you hit a limit

The pricing page does not say what happens when you try to pass the free limits. Each plan lists a fixed number of servers, sites, and deployments, so you must move to a higher plan to get more.

New accounts also get a 5-day free trial of the Pro plan with no credit card. After the trial ends, Ploi sets your subscription to the Free plan and tells you in time to upgrade. Ploi accepts other payment methods besides cards.

## Gotchas

- You pay your cloud provider for the server itself. Ploi's price covers only the panel.
- The Free plan allows 5 deployments a month, so frequent deploys need a paid plan.
- The Free plan lacks features such as team management, site monitoring, and status pages, which the plan table lists for paid plans.

## Questions people ask

### Is Ploi free?

Yes, there is a Free plan with 1 server, 1 site, and 5 deployments per month. There is also a 5-day free trial of the Pro plan.

### Does Ploi need a credit card?

No. The pricing page says the 5-day trial needs no credit card. Ploi also accepts other payment methods if you do not have one.

### Does the Ploi free plan include a server?

No. Ploi's pricing does not include server fees. You connect a cloud provider account and pay that provider for the server.
